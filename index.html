After a week of failures, Washington and Microsoft say the outages aren't over

A national flight halt, a days-long email blackout, and a bank lockout in one week — and the officials watching the systems say more breakdowns are coming.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 1 min read

Read the full story →

Illustration: Wire Willow. A week of failures hit flights, email, and banking — and officials say more are coming.

The story in 30 seconds

  • The week: The FAA halted departures nationwide, Microsoft's Outlook stayed dark for days, and JPMorgan customers were locked out of their accounts — all within about a week.
  • The warning: Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN the public should be prepared for more investment in cybersecurity and more bumps as technology changes; Microsoft president Brad Smith told CBS to expect months of disruption.
  • The cause: The week's failures were believed to be cyberattacks, and Smith said AI has made it easier to find weaknesses in software.
  • The pattern: Attacks on government and commercial systems have picked up, with the Agriculture Department, JPMorgan, and supermarket chains among the targets.

Anthropic's day two: down 8.5 percent, and $109 billion gone

Anthropic's record IPO met reality on its second day of trading. Shares closed Friday, November 20, at $88.91, down 8.5 percent, after trading as low as $83.05 — five cents above the $83 IPO price — shedding about $109 billion in market value in a day, according to The Wall Street Journal. The slide came as the Nasdaq fell about 5.3 percent on Friday in a broad AI sell-off the Journal says sealed a $4 trillion wipeout of market value in the Nasdaq that week. Retail buyers who chased Thursday's open at $111.50 were sitting on losses of roughly 20 percent by Friday's close, and the Journal reports the offering allocated less than 20 percent of shares to retail investors. Anthropic closed its first session Thursday at $97.17, up 17 percent. The IPO itself closed Friday with the underwriters exercising their full option for 147 million additional shares, bringing gross proceeds to about $93.5 billion. On December 15, another roughly 300 million shares become available to trade, which could pressure the stock further.

Sources: Wall Street Journal, Nov 20 · The Tech Hacker, Nov 20

Google puts Genie 4 on the Gemini API — a world you can walk through, at about 10 cents a second

Google made its interactive world model Genie 4 available to developers through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio on November 19, a day after it opened in Project Genie for eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers. Starting from a text prompt, a reference image, or both, developers can generate continuous 720p worlds at 24 frames per second with synchronized native audio — ambient soundscapes, sound effects, and character dialogue — and steer them with keyboard controls and new text prompts as they unfold. Every output carries an imperceptible SynthID watermark. Sessions are capped at 120 seconds, and Google's own documentation warns that object permanence is strongest in roughly the first 45 to 90 seconds — turn away and a building may have moved when you turn back. Access is paid only: a third-party breakdown of Google's pricing page puts the cost at roughly $0.10 per second, about $6.05 a minute.

Sources: Google blog, Nov 19 · CoderLegion, Nov 20

Amazon's Atlanta drone deliveries started without a public filing

Amazon's Prime Air began drone deliveries from Gwinnett County, metro Atlanta, the weekend of November 14–15, using its MK30 drone to deliver items under 5 pounds within 7.5 miles in under an hour, for fees of $2.99 to $4.99, creating about 30 local jobs. But unlike Wing, the Alphabet-owned drone delivery company that also serves the Atlanta area, Amazon's launch left no public environmental review document on the FAA's drone index for Georgia. Wing went through a site-specific environmental assessment with a public comment period that drew zero comments during its July-to-August review window. Amazon, by contrast, relied on a national environmental finding the FAA signed in 2025 covering its Part 135 drone delivery operations broadly — a blanket approval that lets individual city launches proceed through internal FAA review without triggering a new public notice. Amazon has issued no official newsroom post announcing the Atlanta launch; it was first reported by local Atlanta outlets.

Sources: The Robotics Media, Nov 18 · DroneXL, Nov 18
