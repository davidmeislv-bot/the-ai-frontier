ChatGPT went down for three and a half hours on Saturday, and OpenAI's own numbers show how bad it got. Between 1:48 and 5:21 p.m. PT on November 21, requests to ChatGPT and the company's API failed at rates peaking at 75 percent, hitting paid and free users alike. The incident lasted 211 minutes, and OpenAI published a full post-incident write-up on its status page the same day.

The cause, by OpenAI's own account, was about as ordinary as it gets: routine database maintenance. "This outage was triggered by maintenance on a PostgresDB service used by OpenAI's Primary API service that caused both read replicas to fail at the same time," the company wrote.

The reason a database replica took down the whole product is that the replicas were load-bearing. The Primary API service provides user authentication and service-to-service authentication across the entire OpenAI stack, so when both read replicas went offline, the login and inter-service handshake underneath everything else went with them.

The outage swept across nearly every endpoint OpenAI runs. On the API side: Chat Completions, Fine-tuning, Embeddings, Images, Batch, Audio, Moderations, Realtime, and the Files endpoint. Inside ChatGPT it hit Conversations, Login, Search, file uploads, and Voice mode. In practical terms, users could not log in, could not start or continue conversations, could not search, and could not upload files.

Engineers restored service by working with the service provider to bring two new replicas online in the same region, and verified the diagnosis by directing 100 percent of queries at the primary database instance, confirming the replicas — not the primary — were the problem. OpenAI says it has placed a moratorium on similar maintenance, retuned its database to reduce connection churn, and shipped a feature flag that can shift traffic away from replicas by percentage. All systems were back by Saturday evening and showed operational on Sunday morning, November 22.

The breadth of the failure is the story: a single maintenance job on one database service reached every product because they all depend on the same authentication layer — and the redundancy meant to protect it, the second read replica, failed in the same window.
