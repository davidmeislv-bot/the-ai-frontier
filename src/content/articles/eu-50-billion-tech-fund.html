The European Commission has proposed a €50 billion fund to back European companies in artificial intelligence, quantum computing and other strategic technologies.

The Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, unveiled on Wednesday alongside a draft Quantum Act, would target a scale of €50 billion — about $55 billion — and aim to make Europe a global leader in quantum technology by 2030. "We want to build a fund and our target scale is now 50 billion euros," said Henna Virkkunen, the Commission's executive vice-president for tech sovereignty, calling it a "sufficient starting point."

The fund would sit above the start-ups and scale-ups that existing EU funds already back. It is meant for larger companies and large investments in tech infrastructure, including chips, cloud computing, AI and quantum computing, and would complement existing financing programs. The Commission aims to draw in up to €6 of private money for every €1 of public money it commits.

There is a catch: the €50 billion is a target, not money set aside. The Quantum Act carries no budget of its own, and the money would depend on the EU's next long-term budget, which runs from 2028 to 2034 and is still being negotiated. The draft law must also be agreed with EU countries and the European Parliament before it can become law, and operational details are not expected until February 2027.

At €50 billion, the fund would be roughly ten times the €5 billion Scaleup Europe Fund. The Commission presented the plan as part of a wider push to cut Europe's dependence on the United States and China in advanced technologies.
