S&P Global Ratings has put Nvidia's credit rating on watch for a possible downgrade, saying the company's leadership turmoil exposed weaknesses in how it is run.

The agency placed Nvidia's AA issuer rating and the AA rating on its senior unsecured debt on CreditWatch with negative implications on Wednesday, and said it expects to decide within 90 days. It expects any cut to be limited to one notch, to AA-, the fourth-highest grade on its scale, unless investigations or disclosures lead it to conclude that Nvidia's reputation or future business has been harmed.

S&P cited concerns about the independence of Nvidia's board, weak oversight of senior executives, management of geopolitical risks and the company's lack of succession planning. It said the long tenure of some directors may have compromised their independence, and that governance practices "may also not have kept pace with Nvidia's rapid growth." The agency lowered its assessment of Nvidia's management and governance to neutral from positive.

Chief executive Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 and was reinstated by the board on November 23, less than two weeks later. S&P said that sequence did not resolve its questions about how the board oversees executive conduct, and that it was not aware of any steps the board has taken to fix what could be meaningful shortcomings. Nvidia has announced several measures, including forming a committee focused on national security and export controls, launching an investigation into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation, and searching for a chief compliance officer.

The potential downgrade is not a judgment on Nvidia's finances. S&P said its view of the company's competitive position, technological leadership and financial profile is unchanged, and Moody's kept its Aa1 rating and stable outlook on the same day. But the agency warned that stricter US export controls could limit Nvidia's future growth, and that evidence the company circumvented export restrictions or understated its exposure to China would weigh on its view of Nvidia's credit quality.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who briefly served as interim chief executive, told investors the company continued to enjoy strong support from its suppliers and partners.
