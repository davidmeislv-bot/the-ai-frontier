Six years after SpaceX opened Starlink to the public, the cheapest way onto its satellite internet costs less per month than it did on day one — but the price has moved in both directions along the way, and it changed twice in 2026 alone.

When the public beta began in October 2020, the hardware kit cost $499 and the monthly subscription was $99. That put the cost of getting started at $598 before shipping and taxes, and a full first year at $1,687 if the rate had held. Early customers were told to expect download speeds of 50 to 150 megabits per second, latency of 20 to 40 milliseconds, and stretches with no connection at all. SpaceX called the trial the "Better Than Nothing Beta," and the name was accurate: the kit came with the original round dish, a tripod, and a Wi-Fi router, and buyers owned the equipment outright.

The first increase came in March 2022, when the monthly rate rose to $110 and the company pointed to inflation. New hardware went to $599, while customers who already had deposits paid $549. A year later, pricing split by location: $90 a month in areas with spare network capacity, $120 where capacity was tight.

Today's lineup has three home tiers, all with unlimited data. The entry plan is $55 a month for speeds up to 100 megabits per second. The middle tier is $85 for up to 200 megabits. The top tier, Residential Max, is $130, gets priority on the network, and SpaceX says it can reach 400 megabits per second or more where conditions allow. The first two tiers are capped at their stated speeds.

Those prices are not the ones the year started with. In May 2026, SpaceX raised the three tiers by $5, $5, and $10 respectively. In October, it announced further changes based on location: some Max customers were told their bills would climb to $156 a month, while others were told theirs would fall. PCMag reported that some new subscribers were offered up to 50 percent off in a promotion running through November 30, 2026, and Starlink has said certain discounts stay in place as long as the subscription remains active.

Buying the dish outright is no longer the only option. Starlink now rents equipment in several regions. Ars Technica confirmed the company introduced a $10 monthly rental fee in June 2026, which was reportedly waived for some new orders the following month. Renters do not own the hardware: the full kit has to go back within 30 days of canceling, or the subscriber pays its retail price.

The bottom line is that the entry plan now runs $44 a month — $528 a year — below the 2020 subscription price. That still leaves Starlink more expensive than cable, fiber, or 5G home internet in places where those exist; its case has always been coverage where nothing else reaches. For people on the move, the portable Roam plan is $55 a month for 100 gigabytes of high-speed data before speeds drop, and $175 a month for unlimited.

One wrinkle for anyone comparing bills: rates, discounts, and promotions vary by location, and promotional pricing ends when the offer expires or, in some cases, when the customer switches plans. Two households can pay very different amounts for the same service.
