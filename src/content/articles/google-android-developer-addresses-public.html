Google will soon publish the phone numbers and home addresses of many Android app developers for anyone to see.

Starting March 1, 2027, Google will display the contact details of developers who declare themselves traders under British and European law, and users everywhere will be able to see them — not only people in Britain and Europe. Developers must make the declaration by February 1, 2027. Google says the change is needed to comply with the European Union's Digital Services Act and British consumer law, as Google Play shifts to an online marketplace model in Europe and Britain.

The rule covers developers who earn money from their apps, including paid apps, apps with advertising and apps with in-app purchases, especially where the revenue is regular or at high volume. Developers whose apps are only a hobby, or who do not publish in Britain or Europe, can declare themselves non-traders and are exempt.

The problem for small developers is the address itself. Google does not accept a post office box, and the address must match the verified address on the developer's payment profile — which, for a solo developer working from home, is a home address. One independent developer told the Korean outlet The Elec they were considering renting a private office solely to comply.

Apple faces the same European rules but handles them differently: it lets developers supply a post office box address and limits the public display to European countries. Google's version goes worldwide.

Google told Engadget it is "exploring ways to minimize the developer information displayed publicly" while making sure users can still easily reach developers for support.
