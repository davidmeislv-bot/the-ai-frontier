An Amazon delivery drone did its job and then crashed anyway. The Prime Air drone dropped off a bottle of shampoo in a Detroit suburb on Friday afternoon, hit a fiber-optic cable as it took off, lost a propeller, tangled itself in a power line, and came down on the neighbor’s garage.

The crash happened at about 4:30 p.m. on Friday, November 20, in Huntington Woods, Michigan, a suburb north of Detroit. The drone — a Prime Air MK30 weighing more than 80 pounds — had launched from Amazon’s Hazel Park distribution center and delivered the shampoo to Jim Brennan’s backyard shortly after his wife placed the order. It was the Brennans’ first delivery by Prime Air. As Brennan watched it take off, it struck the fiber-optic line and knocked off a propeller, then spiraled, tangled in a power line, and landed on the garage of neighbor Logan Coomes, damaging shingles. No one was injured. Amazon removed the damaged drone from the garage and is in contact with Coomes.

The Federal Aviation Administration is investigating. A preliminary incident report posted to the FAA’s website states the drone “struck a fiber cable, a residential garage, and became entangled in power lines during takeoff,” and lists the drone’s condition as unknown. An FAA spokesman confirmed the investigation to the Detroit Free Press on Monday. The National Transportation Safety Board said on Sunday that it was aware of the incident and gathering information, but had not decided whether to open its own investigation. Huntington Woods public safety said the NTSB “will be conducting the primary investigatory follow up.”

Amazon said safety is its top priority and that such incidents are extremely rare. “Anytime a delivery does not go as planned we investigate what happened and use the findings to further improve the service,” spokesman Jack Stelzner said.

The crash is the first publicly reported one in southeast Michigan since Amazon rolled out Prime Air service last year at distribution centers in Hazel Park and Pontiac. Amazon’s delivery drones operate under an FAA exemption allowing autonomous flights beyond an operator’s line of sight. The agency has investigated earlier Prime Air crashes alongside the NTSB, including two MK30s that struck a construction crane in Arizona in October 2025. Amazon announced in August that it plans to offer autonomous deliveries in nearly 500 cities and towns by the end of 2026, and to reach half a billion customers a year by the end of the decade.
