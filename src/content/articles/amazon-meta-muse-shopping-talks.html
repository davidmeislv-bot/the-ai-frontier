Amazon and Meta are talking about letting Meta's AI agent, Muse, shop on Amazon again.

Fortune reported on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the discussions, that the two companies have held multiple rounds of talks about a deal that would connect Muse directly to Amazon's store. Two issues are still under negotiation: how search results would be presented, and how advertising would be handled under any agreement. Any deal would reportedly need approval from both companies' chief executives, Mark Zuckerberg and Andy Jassy. Neither company has commented, and no deal has been reached.

The talks would reverse a blockade Amazon imposed in September, about two months ago, when Muse users found they could not buy products on Amazon and got an error message at checkout instead. Amazon said Meta had not sought permission before letting the agent crawl its store and accused it of disguising Muse as a regular user.

Amazon's biggest worry is money. Third-party shopping agents like Muse could bypass the paid advertisements Amazon shows shoppers, and executives are concerned they could also weaken Amazon's direct relationship with customers. Chief executive Andy Jassy argued in October that few customers were buying through outside shopping agents. Amazon also worries about what happens when an agent gets an order wrong: the retailer, not Meta, would be left dealing with frustrated customers and unhappy sellers.

The timing matters. The talks come as Amazon heads into Black Friday and Cyber Monday, its busiest shopping stretch of the year. Walmart announced on Tuesday that it would offer its Black Friday deals through Muse, letting shoppers link their Walmart accounts and complete purchases inside the agent. Data from e-commerce firm Forter showed Muse handled 51 percent of AI agent shopping orders in the week ended November 22.
