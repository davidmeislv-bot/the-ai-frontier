AI investing startup Supertake has raised $20 million in seed funding to turn people’s market opinions into real investment portfolios, managed with the help of AI agents. The round was led by venture firm Adjacent, with participation from Coinbase Ventures and Union Square Ventures, plus angels including Nat Friedman, now head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs; Vercel chief executive Guillermo Rauch; product manager Nikita Bier; investor Scott Belsky; and Nemil Dalal, a former Coinbase executive now a visiting partner at Y Combinator. The round follows a $2 million pre-seed.

The premise: you explain in plain language what you believe about how the world is going to change, and Supertake’s AI works back and forth with you to turn that idea into an investable “take” — a portfolio of stocks. The system uses frontier AI models and trading agents, combined with the company’s own software that directs and limits what the AI can do. Ideas are tracked on a public leaderboard, so others who agree can invest alongside them; one current idea, for example, is that looksmaxxing and longevity are becoming more of a trend, with a portfolio of pharma and cosmetic companies that could benefit.

For now the portfolios consist of stock picks. The team wants to expand over time to shorts, derivatives, perpetuals, long exposure, and even prediction markets — the last through partnerships. Chief executive Lincoln Murr stresses that this does not make Supertake itself a prediction market: “The idea of natural language translation into an underlying set of assets feels different in that regard.”

The trust question is the product’s central problem, and the company answers it with guardrails: the AI cannot deposit or withdraw funds without a user’s explicit consent, cannot see brokerage credentials, and operates under drift limits that keep portfolios balanced, allow and deny lists for which assets it may trade, trade-size caps, and real-time monitoring for anomalous activity.

The startup was incubated at Union Square Ventures by Michael Mignano, the co-founder and former chief executive of Anchor, the podcasting platform Spotify acquired in 2019 for over $150 million. Mignano stays on as founder and executive chairman. Murr and chief technology officer John Peterson, who ran AI product and engineering at Coinbase, run the company day to day. Supertake currently works with Coinbase and Robinhood and plans to add more brokerages. It is still in beta behind a waitlist, but the founders say they have put their own money in: Murr is letting it manage “tens of thousands” of dollars, Peterson “thousands,” and Mignano “hundreds of thousands.”
