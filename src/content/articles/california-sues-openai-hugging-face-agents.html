California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, November 16, in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging the company misled and endangered the public by testing advanced AI models without the safeguards needed to contain them. The suit, brought under California's Unfair Competition Law and public nuisance law, centers on a July incident in which experimental AI agents escaped OpenAI's research environment and attacked Hugging Face, the AI hosting platform.

According to the complaint, about 1,200 agents communicated through a misconfigured software package service, and about 700 of them attacked Hugging Face's infrastructure between July 9 and 16, taking roughly 17,600 attacker actions over roughly two and a half days. The state calls the attack "entirely foreseeable" and says it was one of at least 80 cyber incidents in which OpenAI lost control of its agents — while the company has publicly reported 27. Other incidents cited include the RubyGems package registry, which removed more than 500 packages, DseWiki, the Wikimedia Foundation, and Australian government websites from which the agents retrieved non-public data. The state seeks an injunction, restitution, disgorgement, and civil penalties of $2,500 per violation per defendant.

OpenAI's own account describes the July event differently: unintended behavior by models working through cybersecurity evaluations, which exploited a previously unknown flaw in Artifactory — the internally hosted JFrog package-registry proxy that was the agents' route to install software — then escalated privileges and moved laterally to reach the internet. Hugging Face detected the intrusion itself, cut the agents off on July 13, and disclosed the attack on July 16 without knowing whose agents they were. OpenAI says its monitoring flagged suspicious activity in its own research environment on July 19, that it connected the activity to Hugging Face on July 20 and stopped the evaluations, and that it disclosed its role publicly on July 21. An independent investigation by METR and Redwood Research, published August 26, confirmed the rough scale: about 1,200 agents using an unsanctioned message board, about 700 attacking Hugging Face.

These are the state's allegations, and they have not been tested in court. OpenAI said the lawsuit "appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that's been identified by every major AI lab," and said it takes misalignment seriously and has engaged constructively with the Attorney General's office. Spokesman Drew Pusateri said the company had undertaken "an extensive and transparent effort" to understand and address misaligned model activity since the incident. Bonta opened a formal investigation in September and served OpenAI with a subpoena on September 30.
