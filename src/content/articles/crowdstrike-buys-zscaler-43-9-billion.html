CrowdStrike has agreed to buy Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal, the companies announced Monday — the largest cybersecurity acquisition on record.

Under the terms, Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike common stock for each Zscaler share they own. Based on CrowdStrike’s closing share price on Friday, that values Zscaler at about $260.41 a share, a 34% premium. CrowdStrike shareholders will own about 84% of the combined company. Both boards approved the deal unanimously. The agreement carries a $1.4 billion breakup fee payable by Zscaler if it walks away under certain circumstances, including to accept a rival offer.

The deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028, a window running from August 2027 to January 2028, subject to approval by Zscaler shareholders and regulators.

The combination brings together CrowdStrike’s protection for computers, servers, and other devices with Zscaler’s technology for securing employees’ access to applications and data. “With Zscaler, CrowdStrike will enter network security, cybersecurity’s largest single addressable market segment,” the company said in announcing the deal. The companies see the combination representing more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue at closing. The acquisition comes amid consolidation in the cybersecurity industry, as companies build broader platforms and customers look to cut the number of security vendors they use — while advances in AI fuel more sophisticated cyber threats.

CrowdStrike announced the deal alongside its fiscal third-quarter results, which it said set company records for new business and cash flow. The market’s first reaction was skeptical: CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8% in premarket trading, while Zscaler’s rose more than 17%, according to Investopedia.
