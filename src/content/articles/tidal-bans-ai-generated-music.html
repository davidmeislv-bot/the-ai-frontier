Tidal will become the first major streaming service to ban wholly AI-generated music outright, the company announced on Tuesday, and the purge will be large: about 29 million tracks removed, with 80,000 to 85,000 new submissions rejected every day after that.

The removals start December 9. Platform lead Robert Anderson said in a post on X that the tracks will be “completely gone by the beginning of the year.” The policy covers only songs generated solely by AI tools. Music made with some AI assistance stays, and Tidal will take a track down if a distributor discloses it was made solely with generative AI or if Tidal’s own detection systems identify it as fully AI-generated. The detection comes from Ircam Amplify, a company affiliated with the French music institute IRCAM.

The scale of the problem is what forced the move. Over the last 90 days, 43 percent of all tracks distributed to Tidal were detected as AI-generated, according to the company. About 27 million tracks in the catalog — roughly 6 percent of it — are currently labeled wholly AI-generated, and Anderson said 96 percent of all AI-generated songs receive zero plays. Earlier this year Tidal demonetized fully AI-generated tracks and labeled every such song as AI-generated or AI-assisted. On Tuesday, Anderson said that had not been enough: “Something more drastic needs to be done.”

Tidal is the first streaming service to take the ban this far, and the only other platform with an outright ban is Bandcamp, which bars music “generated wholly or in substantial part by AI.” Spotify has taken a different route, saying in October it would keep removing “slop and spam” from recommendations while also letting people remix and cover songs using AI. SoundCloud has partnered with AI music companies ElevenLabs and Moises. Deezer added a toggle to hide AI-made music without deleting it, and Apple Music labels songs made with AI.

That Tidal moved first is a curiosity of ownership. Block, the company that has owned Tidal since 2021, is led by Jack Dorsey, an outspoken AI enthusiast. At the start of the year Block laid off 4,000 of its 10,000 employees, with Dorsey attributing the decision entirely to AI. Anderson said Tidal is “the first DSP to do so, and we encourage our peers to follow suit.”
