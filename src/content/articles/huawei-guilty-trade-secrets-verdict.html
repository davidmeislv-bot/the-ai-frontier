A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei on Tuesday of conspiring to steal trade secrets from American companies and defrauding banks, closing a criminal case that has run since 2018.

Jurors found Huawei Technologies guilty on 10 of the 12 counts it faced, including bank fraud, wire fraud, conspiracy to steal trade secrets, sanctions violations and conspiracy to commit money laundering. Its three subsidiaries — Huawei Device Co. Ltd., Futurewei Technologies Inc. and Huawei Device USA Inc. — were convicted on two counts each, conspiracy to steal trade secrets and conspiracy to commit wire fraud. The jury acquitted the company on the two remaining counts: racketeering conspiracy and conspiracy to obstruct justice.

Sentencing is set for September 14, 2027, before US District Judge Ann Donnelly. The company faces criminal fines, forfeiture and restitution that could run into the billions of dollars.

Prosecutors argued that Huawei ran a decades-long scheme on two tracks: taking technology from American companies, and hiding business in Iran from the banks that handled its money. The trial evidence included source code taken from Cisco Systems for its routers, antenna technology from wireless company Quintel, a circuit board design from chip company CNEX, and T-Mobile's phone-testing robot, known as Tappy. Prosecutors said Huawei misled HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas about its Iranian business, which it ran through a company called Skycom.

The case began with a 2018 indictment and expanded in 2020. The United States placed Huawei on a trade blacklist in 2019, restricting its access to American technology, and Huawei chief financial officer Meng Wanzhou was arrested in Canada on a US warrant in December 2018. The trial itself ran more than two months, with prosecutors calling more than 60 witnesses and the defense calling 15.

Huawei's lawyer, Brian Heberlig, said the company would appeal the convictions but was heartened by the acquittals, calling the racketeering acquittal a vindication of its compliance culture. In a statement on Wednesday, the company welcomed the racketeering acquittal and disputed the trade-secret findings, arguing the five disputes behind them were civil matters resolved over the past two decades.
