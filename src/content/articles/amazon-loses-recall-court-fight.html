A federal judge has upheld orders making Amazon recall more than 400,000 hazardous products sold by other sellers through its Fulfillment by Amazon program.

U.S. District Judge Lydia Kay Griggsby of the District of Maryland ruled on Tuesday, granting summary judgment to the Consumer Product Safety Commission and dismissing Amazon's challenge to the agency's orders. The commission announced the decision on Wednesday.

The products at issue are faulty carbon monoxide detectors that fail to alarm, hair dryers sold without the immersion protection that guards against electrocution if the dryer falls into water, and children's sleepwear that fails federal flammability standards. The commission says about 24,000 of the detectors and nearly 400,000 of the hair dryers are covered.

The case turns on whether Amazon counts as a distributor when it stores, packs and ships goods sold by others. In July 2024 the commission unanimously found that it does, because Amazon handles the goods and the transaction through Fulfillment by Amazon, and in January 2025 it ordered the company to run the recalls. Amazon sued to challenge both orders. The court rejected that challenge and left the orders in place, though the judge's reasoning is not yet public: the memorandum opinion was filed under seal, with the parties due to propose redactions by December 8.

Under the commission's order, Amazon must post recall notices on its site the same day the commission publishes them, notify the third-party sellers, and give a full refund to any buyer who shows the product was destroyed or disposed of. Amazon told The Verge it has "a longstanding commitment to removing unsafe and recalled products from our store and notifying customers" and said it is reviewing its options after the ruling. It is not yet clear whether the company will appeal.
