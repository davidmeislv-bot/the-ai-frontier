Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia chief executive is headed for a public hearing. The Senate Banking Committee will question him in public on December 3, committee chair Senator Tim Scott told Bloomberg, with the session focused on oversight of U.S. export controls. That hearing follows the closed-door testimony before three House committees already scheduled for December 1.

The committee first asked Huang to testify after he admitted to an affair. Reports have linked him with an executive at a company to which Nvidia is said to have sold more than $4 billion worth of chips and other equipment. U.S. officials suspect that executive, Alice Huang, of working for Chinese intelligence. Since 2024, the Commerce and Justice departments have been investigating Megaspeed, the Singaporean cloud company where she worked between 2023 and 2025. She is also reportedly under investigation over alleged links to Chinese AI companies that may have broken U.S. export rules by smuggling in chips from Nvidia and Super Micro Computer.

Nvidia set up a board-level export control compliance committee last week and said on Monday it is looking to appoint someone to a new Chief Compliance Officer role. Both the company’s internal investigation and the Banking Committee’s inquiry are still open.

The political reaction was swift and crossed party lines. Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi called the reinstatement “outrageous,” saying neither the government nor Nvidia’s board has completed its investigation. Senator Chris Coons called it “reckless,” writing that it confirmed fears the administration had “sacrificed our national security to let China get what it wants most: access to our advanced chips.” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said the rushed reinstatement “underscores the need for Congress to act.” Senator Adam Schiff said Congress “must conduct a thorough investigation of both NVIDIA and the Trump Administration over chip sales and diversions to China.”

Republicans were critical too. Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich wrote that there was “something profoundly wrong” about the affair and the chip sales. Representative Don Bacon said Huang “was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy” and that Nvidia should have moved on. Senator John Curtis asked which government officials gave the board input, what they said, and on what basis the board concluded the return posed no national security risk.

Not everyone objected. Former presidential candidate Andrew Yang wrote: “The truth is that Nvidia needs Jensen Huang and vice versa. The sherriff of the AI trade is back after a brief break.”

President Donald Trump, who said he convinced the board to reappoint Huang, posted an AI-generated meme of the chief executive holding a woman’s bra, an image recalling the famous photo from Trump’s own assassination attempt.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, the committee’s ranking Democrat, said the return “does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives and the company’s board still owe Congress answers about the company’s compliance with US export controls,” and that the administration needs to explain what “input” senior officials gave and who gave it. Warren and colleagues from both parties are pushing legislation that would tighten chip export rules: the AI Overwatch Act would write the administration’s limits on chip exports to “foreign adversaries” into law, the Chip Security Act would require Nvidia and other companies to track the locations of chips they send abroad, and the MATCH Act would strengthen semiconductor export controls for the U.S. and its allies. According to Bloomberg, final talks are underway to fold the bills into the annual defense package lawmakers usually pass in December.

Investors, meanwhile, kept buying. Nvidia shares closed up 31.5% at $165.05 on Monday, after losing about a third of their value over the previous two weeks.
