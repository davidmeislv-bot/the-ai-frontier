Chinese carmaker XPeng has unveiled a humanoid robot it says is ready for mass production.

The company introduced XPENG IRON at its AI Day event in Guangzhou on Wednesday, where the robot made its first public live appearance: it walked onto the stage, spoke into a handheld microphone and answered unscripted questions from the audience. XPeng said hardware mass production will begin in January 2027.

IRON has 76 degrees of freedom, including a 21-degree-of-freedom dexterous hand, and more than 1,500 tactile sensing points covering over 80 percent of key body areas. Its palms warm to between 25 and 40 degrees Celsius to feel more natural during human contact, and it has 3D vision and 360-degree perception of its surroundings. Three of XPeng's own Turing AI chips deliver 2,250 TOPS of computing power to run its onboard models and whole-body motion control.

The robot runs on IRON OS under XPeng's new Turing Physical AI Architecture, also called VLA 3.0, which the company presents as a shared brain for its cars, robotaxis, flying vehicles and humanoids. To train it, XPeng operates a 50,000-square-meter Robot Data Factory covering more than 250 scenarios across 15 job categories.

The factory is built for volume. XPeng said its automated assembly line has more than 600 AI-assisted holographic quality checkpoints and puts each unit through more than 1,000 vehicle-grade tests, and is engineered to produce one humanoid every 2.5 minutes at full capacity. IRON will begin training as a store assistant in XPeng retail stores in the first quarter of 2027, handling customer reception and product guidance, with a public release targeted for the second quarter.

The company also showed its purpose-built YOYO-01 robotaxi, scheduled for 2027, and said driverless passenger testing has already begun in Guangzhou.

All specifications are the company's claims: XPeng disclosed no price, height, weight or battery capacity for IRON.
