China has prohibited SoftBank from acquiring ABB's robotics business in China, blocking a central piece of the Japanese firm's push into physical AI. SoftBank was notified on November 4 by China's Office of the Working Mechanism for Foreign Investment Security Review; ABB confirmed the decision on November 5. The signed deal, agreed in October 2025, valued ABB's robotics division at $5.375 billion. The unit employs about 7,000 people and generated about $2.3 billion in revenue in 2024.

Neither company disclosed the regulator's reasoning. The Financial Times reported concerns that robotics data generated in China could be sent abroad and used to train AI models, and that SoftBank's close ties with OpenAI weighed on the review. ABB did not confirm that explanation, and Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun directed questions to the relevant Chinese authorities. Both companies say they are working constructively on the transaction, and neither has confirmed a carve-out or a revised price. The prohibition covers the China business specifically; it does not formally terminate the worldwide deal.

The stakes are large because China is the world's biggest industrial robot market — 354,000 industrial robots installed in 2025, 59 percent of the world total, according to the International Federation of Robotics — and ABB Robotics' largest market, at roughly 30 percent of the division's business, according to press reports. The European Commission cleared the deal unconditionally in March; American antitrust review was still pending when China's decision landed. ABB chief executive Morten Wierod has said SoftBank, not ABB, leads the talks on securing approvals, and called for patience.
