Dell doubled its revenue in a single quarter on demand for AI servers — and the market still sent its shares down.

The company reported third-quarter revenue of $54.94 billion, up 103 percent from a year earlier and well above the $49.32 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings came in at $9.45 a share, against expectations of $6.60, and net income reached $5.67 billion.

The growth came from the infrastructure group, where revenue nearly tripled to about $42 billion, generating $7.1 billion in operating income. Sales of AI-optimized servers jumped to $22.03 billion from $5.64 billion a year earlier, up 291 percent, while traditional server and networking revenue rose to $15.01 billion from $4.48 billion. The PC business grew just 3 percent, to $12.9 billion.

Dell booked $60.2 billion in new AI orders during the quarter and ended it with a record $132.3 billion backlog of unbuilt AI servers. More than 7,500 customers are now deploying its AI Factory bundles of servers, storage, networking and software. Chief operating officer Jeff Clarke said the company is now a different one, with the infrastructure group driving most of its profit and growth.

Dell raised its full-year forecast for the third time this year, lifting expected revenue to $202 billion from $192 billion, adjusted earnings to $29.68 a share from $25.50, and AI server revenue to $78 billion from $74 billion. It expects about $56.3 billion in revenue in the current quarter. The board also approved a $25 billion expansion of its share buyback program.

The stock still fell more than 7 percent in premarket trading on Wednesday, as investors weighed the cost of building AI infrastructure against what it returns — a theme covered in the next story.
