Volvo Cars has started training humanoid robots on real factory work at its plant in Ghent, Belgium.

The robots are Hexagon Robotics' AEON machines, and Ghent is the first Volvo plant to try humanoids. Volvo's own team is teaching them alongside employees and existing automation, using imitation learning — the robot learns a task by copying how a person performs it. The first job is placing components into two fixtures with high precision.

"AEON's humanoid design means it can fit into our production as it is today, alongside our teams and existing automation," Magnus Nilsson, Volvo's chief manufacturing officer, said in the announcement on Thursday.

AEON stands 1.65 metres tall and weighs 60 kilograms. It uses cameras and sensors to read its surroundings, moves on wheels instead of feet at up to 2.4 metres per second — about the pace of a light jog — and can carry 10 kilograms. Volvo says the aim is to support employees in repetitive and ergonomically challenging work, and the robots are being trained and validated on real tasks before they take on production work.

The schedule is deliberately slow. A wider factory pilot with more AEON units is planned for the first half of 2027, and Volvo aims to have the robots working in real production from mid-2027.
