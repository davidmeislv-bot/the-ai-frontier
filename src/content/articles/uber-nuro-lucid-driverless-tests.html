Uber, Nuro and Lucid have started testing their robotaxis without anyone behind the wheel.

The three companies announced on Tuesday that fully driverless testing has begun on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area, in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale, ahead of a public launch in the first quarter of 2027. The vehicles are Lucid Gravity SUVs fitted with high-resolution cameras, solid-state lidar sensors and radar, running Nuro's Level 4 self-driving system. Lucid has delivered more than 100 vehicles for the program, and the companies said employee rides in driverless vehicles will begin soon.

Nuro has been testing the Gravity robotaxi on Bay Area and Houston roads with a safety driver since December 2025, and received a driverless testing permit from the California Department of Motor Vehicles in May. The program, announced in July 2025, is Uber's largest robotaxi commitment: the company has committed to buying at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles equipped with Nuro's system over six years, up from the 20,000 it originally promised. Uber has also put $500 million into Lucid, up from the $300 million first agreed, and nearly $500 million into Nuro. Uber will own and operate the fleet, and passengers will summon rides through the Uber app.

The public launch, once planned for this year, is now set for the first quarter of 2027, with Houston to follow later in the year and dozens of markets planned after that. Nuro still needs a separate permit from California's utilities commission before it can charge passengers for driverless rides.
