Australian AI infrastructure firm Firmus is reportedly seeking about $1.5 billion in a bond offering, part of a larger debt package that could reach up to $4 billion. The company, backed by Nvidia, held talks with banks and investors last week, and a bond could come before the end of the year, alongside a separate bank loan. Both would run for 69 months; the bond is expected to yield around 8%, and the loan margin about 325 basis points over the secured overnight financing rate. Morgan Stanley is leading the debt financing.

The money would go mainly toward buying Nvidia’s latest GB300 AI chips for Firmus’s data-center project in Batam, Indonesia, developed with DayOne Data Centers — a campus designed for up to 170,000 graphics processors, projected to generate $25–30 billion in offtake revenue over six years.

The debt plan follows a change of course. Firmus shelved its Australian stock-exchange listing in October — it had sought up to $5.5 billion including the greenshoe — and is now seeking about $3 billion through equity, debt, and customer prepayments. Its most recent private round raised $2 billion at a $10.5 billion post-money valuation, backed by Nvidia, Coatue, Blackstone, and Jane Street. Negotiations with Nvidia and Meta are reportedly at advanced stages.

One caution: the deal rests on a single source — Bloomberg’s reporting — and nothing is final. Terms and timing could still change.
