X has added its first parental controls for teenagers, twenty years after the service launched as Twitter.

Accounts belonging to 13-to-17-year-olds now default to a protected mode that restricts sensitive media and blocks targeted advertising. On top of that, parents and guardians can see how much time their teen spends on X each week and set a daily limit, require approval for new followers, limit the For You feed and videos to accounts the teen already follows, filter specific words, turn off sensitive media, choose who can send the teen direct messages, and see who the teen is messaging.

There are hard limits on what parents can see. They cannot read their teen's private messages, see search or viewing history, post as their teen, or open the teen's account. Every change a parent makes is recorded and visible to the teen.

The controls only switch on if both sides agree. A parent sends an invitation and the teen must accept it within 72 hours; one parent can supervise up to five teens, and a teen can be linked to only one parent. Supervision ends automatically when the teen turns 18, and a teen can ask to end it sooner, though the parent must approve that request.

"Parents want practical tools, and teens deserve room to grow. With Parental Controls on X, families can set the guardrails that fit their home, while teens keep ownership of their accounts and can see every change. Striking that balance is vital and what we set out to build," Michael O'Herlihy, X's head of safety, wrote in a blog post.

The controls arrive months after X faced criticism over its Grok chatbot being used to generate sexualized images of children and women. X has never been aimed at teenagers, but a 2024 study found nearly a third of Gen Alpha children have an account, and the platform requires users to be at least 13.
