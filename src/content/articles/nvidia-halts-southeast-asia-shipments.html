Nvidia has told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping AI servers to four Southeast Asian countries, the Financial Times reported on Thursday.

The chipmaker instructed partners including Dell and Supermicro, which package Nvidia's chips into servers, to halt shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The partners have told buyers in those countries that their orders will not be shipped, according to the report, which cited three people with knowledge of the matter.

Reuters, which reported the Financial Times story, said it could not immediately verify the report. Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro did not immediately respond to requests for comment outside regular business hours.

It is unclear whether the halt is temporary. The Financial Times reported that Nvidia and its partners are working through how to comply with new US export controls. The controls are part of Washington's effort to limit China's access to advanced chips, and the four countries have drawn scrutiny as possible routes for hardware headed elsewhere.

The halt is the second blow to Nvidia in a day: S&P Global Ratings also placed the company's credit rating on watch for a possible downgrade over governance concerns, covered in the Tech Finance section below.
