The two biggest names in cloud security may soon become one company. CrowdStrike has opened talks to acquire Zscaler, Bloomberg first reported on Friday, and Reuters confirmed the discussions with its own sources. Neither company has confirmed anything, and the people describing the talks say there is no guarantee a deal will happen — only that a combination could come together soon.

Traders moved fast. Zscaler shares jumped 17% in after-hours trading on Friday, while CrowdStrike fell 8%. At Friday's close, Zscaler was worth about $32 billion and CrowdStrike about $235 billion, so a merged company would be valued at roughly $265 billion.

A deal at that scale would rank among the largest cybersecurity transactions ever, alongside Google's $32 billion purchase of Wiz earlier this year, Cisco's roughly $28 billion deal for Splunk, and Palo Alto Networks' $25 billion acquisition of CyberArk.

The pairing makes sense on paper. CrowdStrike's Falcon platform guards the devices — laptops, servers and phones — while Zscaler's software guards the network connections between workers and the applications they use, checking identity at every step instead of trusting anyone inside a corporate network. CrowdStrike has long been thin in network security, and buying Zscaler would close its biggest gap against Palo Alto Networks, the industry's platform leader. Customers, meanwhile, keep asking to buy more security tools from fewer suppliers, and a combined company could sell both halves of the stack in a single contract.

Any deal would probably be paid mostly in CrowdStrike stock. The company ended July with about $5 billion in cash, far short of what Zscaler would cost outright. It would also be a sharp change of scale for CrowdStrike, whose purchases so far have been small — earlier this month it agreed to buy two AI security startups, Fiddler AI and Dreadnode.

One person matters more than any banker here. Jay Chaudhry, Zscaler's founder and chief executive, is also its largest shareholder, so a sale almost certainly needs his support. He started the company in 2007 and built it around the idea that no user or device should be trusted by default, a model the industry now calls zero trust. CrowdStrike was founded in 2011 by George Kurtz and grew into one of the world's largest security companies from its cloud-based endpoint platform.

The talks arrive during a wave of security-industry consolidation. Palo Alto Networks' $25 billion CyberArk deal and Google's $32 billion Wiz purchase are the recent benchmarks, and Bloomberg's analysis puts the wider security market at $244 billion and argues that customers are consolidating vendors. Both companies report earnings within a day of each other in early December — CrowdStrike is expected to report on December 1, Zscaler on December 2 — which gives the talks a natural deadline, or a reason to pause.
