A Florida man has been sentenced to nearly four years in federal prison for using an AI-cloned voice to impersonate a popular streamer in a cryptocurrency scam.

David Anthony Sanchez, 36, of Port St. Lucie, was sentenced to 47 months in prison by U.S. District Judge Melissa Damian, the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida announced on Tuesday. He had pleaded guilty to money laundering and aggravated identity theft, and was also ordered to pay restitution to his victims.

Sanchez set up online accounts made to look as though they belonged to the streamer N3ON, and contacted the organizers of a cryptocurrency memecoin project while posing as both the streamer and his representative. He signed agreements using N3ON's identity and sent the organizers an AI-generated voice recording that mimicked the streamer, falsely confirming that N3ON would endorse, promote and take part in the project.

Believing the deal was real, the organizers sent about $260,000 in cryptocurrency to accounts Sanchez controlled. He then converted much of it into U.S. dollars through transactions designed to hide where the money came from, and spent it on himself, according to prosecutors. The FBI's Miami field office investigated the case.

The sentence is an early sign of how courts treat AI voice cloning as a tool for fraud: the aggravated identity theft count, which carries a mandatory prison term on top of the sentence for the underlying crime, was applied to a voice generated by software rather than a stolen document.
