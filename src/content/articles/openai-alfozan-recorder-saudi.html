Federal prosecutors say the former OpenAI employee charged with hiding a recording device at the company’s headquarters was “in frequent communication with various people affiliated with the Saudi government,” and they have asked a court to deny him bail.

Abdulrahman Alfozan was arrested in October after company staff discovered a recording device behind a television in an executive conference room. He was fired the same day. According to Bloomberg, as reported by Engadget, prosecutors argued in a bail filing that Alfozan might try to return to his native Saudi Arabia because of his weak ties to the United States.

Investigators found hundreds of audio files on Alfozan’s computer, dozens of which were recordings of meetings and conversations that took place at OpenAI throughout September 2026. Many of those recordings involved “what may be trade secrets,” prosecutors said. He uploaded some of them to Dropbox, and allegedly made AI-generated transcripts of the recordings, sorting them by date and renaming them based on their contents. Authorities also apparently found images on his phone of a “strategic partnership” document marked “confidential.”

Alfozan denies that he was stealing the company’s trade secrets for Saudi Arabia, and is asking the court to set a $1 million bond. Bloomberg reports that U.S. District Judge Vince Chhabria is already doubting whether Alfozan should remain in custody without the evidence to support a more serious charge. The court hears his bond request today in San Francisco.
