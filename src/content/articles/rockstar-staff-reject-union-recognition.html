Workers at Rockstar Games' British studios have voted against formal union recognition, rejecting a deal with the Independent Workers Union of Great Britain.

The two-week ballot, which ran from November 12 to 26, covered staff at the company's studios in Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee and London. Of the 1,544 employees who voted — 93.2 percent of those eligible — 1,163 voted against recognition and 381 in favor, a split of 75.3 percent to 24.7 percent.

The vote overlapped with the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI on November 19, which generated $3.79 billion in pre-orders and first-day sales. The IWGB's Rockstar Game Workers Union said it will keep organizing without a formal agreement with the company.

Without recognition, Rockstar is not obliged to bargain with the union over pay, overtime, bonuses, leave or pensions. The result is a setback for one of the most closely watched union campaigns in the British games industry, which had sought a formal footing at the studio behind one of the biggest entertainment launches ever.
