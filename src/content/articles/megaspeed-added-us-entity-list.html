The Commerce Department added Megaspeed to its trade blacklist on Monday, the same day Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. Semafor first reported the move; the Wall Street Journal, the New York Post, and Bloomberg Law later confirmed the listing.

Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based cloud and AI infrastructure provider, is now on the Commerce Department’s Entity List, along with six other entities — Malaysia-based MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International, and Speedmatrix; Singapore-based Apex Enterprise Solutions and Swiftdata; and Thailand-based Megaspeed Co. Ltd. US companies cannot sell goods, software, or technology to a listed company without a special license, which is usually denied. The listing is aimed at stopping Megaspeed from obtaining US-made semiconductors.

The company is at the center of the scandal that briefly cost Huang his job. Its former executive Alice Huang, with whom Huang admitted having an affair, is suspected by US officials of working for Chinese intelligence — an allegation Reuters said it could not independently verify. The New York Times reported last month that US authorities had concluded she passed information to Beijing. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

The Commerce Department opened a probe into Megaspeed in 2024 over possible export control violations, and interviewed Alice Huang in June 2025 — the same month Nvidia put the firm on its internal “no sell” list, according to the New York Post. The Justice Department has since taken the lead on a criminal investigation, and the two departments said in a joint statement last week that they would pursue “the full extent of the law” against the company for any criminal conduct. The Post also reported that a 2025 audit of Megaspeed’s supplies found at least 50,000 advanced AI chips had gone missing. Alice Huang officially left Megaspeed on August 30, 2025, according to Megaspeed.

Nvidia still lists Megaspeed as an official cloud partner in its directory. The blacklisting landed hours after Nvidia’s board voted to reinstate Huang, following pressure from President Trump and other administration officials.
