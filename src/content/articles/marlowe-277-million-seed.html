Former Microsoft executives Dan Lewis and Kutta Srinivasan have raised $277 million for their new startup, Marlowe, on a bet that the next wave of AI will run on a very different kind of data center. The seed round, led by Accel, values the company at $1.15 billion, according to the company — a billion-dollar valuation six months after it was founded in May.

Marlowe designs and operates computing systems for inference — the running of trained models in everyday products — rather than for training them. It starts with chips from AMD and Etched, expanding beyond the Nvidia GPUs that have fueled the AI boom, and tunes chips, networking, and software together for each customer’s workload. The company claims that approach delivers two to three times more output per watt and per dollar than a generic GPU cloud. It has not ruled out using Nvidia chips later.

Its first site, built around Etched’s chips, is scheduled to go live in December and will primarily serve a large AI coding company. A second, larger site using AMD’s new Helios systems is on track for the second quarter of 2027. Much of the funding will go toward chips, networking equipment, and hiring; Marlowe has about 30 employees and is based in Bellevue, Washington, and Redwood City, California.

The $277 million includes a smaller initial round led by Eclipse. Other backers include Bessemer, Altimeter, Atreides, Journey, and Valor, alongside KKR co-founder Henry Kravis, Uber chief executive Dara Khosrowshahi, and Box chief executive Aaron Levie. By the standards of AI infrastructure, $277 million does not go far — but the size of the bet shows how much appetite there is for an alternative to building everything on Nvidia.
