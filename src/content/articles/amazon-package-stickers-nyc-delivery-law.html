Amazon has started putting stickers on its packages to argue against a New York City delivery bill. The stickers warn that changes to last-mile deliveries proposed by the City Council could add “up to $5 per order,” and include a QR code that lets customers contact their representative to protest. The stickers follow in-app notifications warning of potential extra fees.

The target is the Delivery Protection Act, known as Intro 518. High-volume delivery companies, particularly Amazon, rely on contractors rather than employees for last-mile delivery. The bill would create new licensing rules for last-mile operations and require delivery workers to be company employees rather than contractors. In August, New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani announced his backing for the act, which had been introduced several months earlier.

The Teamsters union and other supporters argue the bill would hold companies like Amazon responsible while giving delivery workers better pay and benefits. Amazon says it would lead to higher costs and slower deliveries, and has threatened to move its distribution centers out of New York City to avoid the regulation. One Reddit user, u/SkippyReddit, pushed back on Amazon’s campaign: “New Yorkers support this law. Amazon’s propaganda machine is trying to make us think otherwise.”
