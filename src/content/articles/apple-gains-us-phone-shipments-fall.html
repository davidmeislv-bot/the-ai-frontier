United States smartphone shipments fell 9 percent in the third quarter of 2026, and Apple was the only major maker to come out ahead.

Counterpoint Research reported that rising memory costs squeezed Android manufacturers, hitting low-end phones hardest, while Apple's resilience lifted its share of the American market to 57 percent, up from 50 percent a year earlier. Apple was the only major manufacturer whose shipments did not fall in the quarter.

Samsung held second place with 25 percent of the market, up a point from 24 percent a year ago, helped by a mix of flagship Galaxy S and Z phones and low-end Galaxy A models. Motorola took the biggest hit: its share fell four points to 8 percent as memory costs squeezed its low-end lineup, reversing the gains it made in the second quarter.

Sales through the three biggest American carriers fell 4 percent in the quarter, and Counterpoint expects sales to stay weak in the fourth quarter as well. The firm blames the slump on global memory constraints and soft demand for cheaper Android phones, a squeeze that has raised component costs across the budget end of the market.
