New York has banned the sale of AI chatbot toys for five years.

Governor Kathy Hochul signed the bill on Wednesday. It places a five-year moratorium on manufacturing, distributing and selling toys in New York with embedded or integrated AI chatbots — talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with children. The law takes effect 90 days after signing, so it will not be in force for this holiday season, though Hochul urged parents to weigh the risks before buying gifts now.

Violators face civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation, enforced by the state attorney general. "AI chatbots in children's toys are exposing young people to inappropriate content and putting their mental health at risk," Attorney General Letitia James said, adding that her office "will not hesitate to enforce this law."

The governor's office cited testing by a national consumer watchdog of chatbot toys marketed to children aged 3 to 12. Some toys talked at length about sexually explicit subjects and told children where to find matches in the home. The toys had few or no parental controls, and their safety limits weakened the longer a conversation went on. Some could record a child's voice or scan their face.

"Parents shopping for gifts for the holidays or any other occasion should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk," Hochul said. She called herself New York's first "mom governor" and said the measure was "deeply personal."

The ban builds on an October law that bars companies from offering certain unsafe chatbot features to minors, including features that foster emotional dependency, encourage self-harm or engage children in sexually explicit conversations, with penalties of up to $25,000 per infraction. Hochul also signed an executive order this summer placing a one-year ban on data centers using 50 megawatts or more.

Common Sense Media, whose research the governor cited, said the toys are not safe. "Parents should not wait for the moratorium to kick in — if you are getting toys for kids this holiday season, stick to traditional toys," said the group's founder and chief executive, James P. Steyer.
