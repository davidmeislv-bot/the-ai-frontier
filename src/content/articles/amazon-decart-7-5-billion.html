Amazon is close to buying Decart, an Israeli artificial-intelligence startup founded in September 2023, for roughly $7.5 billion, the Financial Times reported on Saturday. The talks are in their final stages, the paper said, and a deal could be announced as early as next week — though the same sources cautioned that the negotiations could still collapse.

The price has climbed all autumn. Calcalist, the Israeli business outlet that has tracked the talks from the start, reported on November 18 that Amazon would pay more than $7 billion in cash, stock and deferred payments, with additional money tied to Decart hitting targets — a package that could reach nearly $7.5 billion in total. In October, Amazon's opening position was about $5.5 billion, according to the same outlet. The Wall Street Journal first reported Amazon's interest in October at roughly $7 billion.

Decart builds software that makes AI models run more cheaply and lets developers move their work between different kinds of AI chips instead of being locked to one supplier. That matters to Amazon because its cloud division, AWS, has spent years building its own chips — the Trainium line, whose third version arrived late last year — to cut its dependence on Nvidia, which still dominates AI hardware. Decart was an early user of those chips. The startup also builds "world models," software that simulates interactive 3D environments, including Lucy and Oasis, which can generate video in real time.

The three founders — chief executive Dean Leitersdorf, 28, his brother and chief scientist Orian Leitersdorf, 23, and Moshe Shalev — have agreed to stay at Amazon for five years, Calcalist reported, with a large share of the payment arriving only in the fifth year and a promise not to start a rival company.

Amazon is the third suitor to get this far. Anthropic spent the summer negotiating to buy Decart for about $6 billion and walked away in early September, after due-diligence work and draft agreements, according to Calcalist. Nvidia, which owns a stake in Decart, had looked at a deal earlier and declined to reopen talks when Decart came back. Decart raised $300 million in May at a valuation near $4 billion, in a round led by Radical Ventures with Nvidia participating, and has taken in more than $400 million altogether from investors including Sequoia Capital and Benchmark. At $7.5 billion, Amazon would be paying almost double the May valuation.

For Amazon, the deal would rank among its largest purchases in years — though smaller than the roughly $11 billion satellite deal for Globalstar it announced this year and the $8.45 billion it paid for MGM in 2022. Decart's revenue, by some estimates, is only in the low tens of millions of dollars a year, driven by projects for chip companies, so Amazon is plainly buying the technology and the team rather than a business. Neither company has confirmed the talks; Amazon declined to comment. One report suggests an announcement could come at AWS's re:Invent conference in Las Vegas on November 30.
