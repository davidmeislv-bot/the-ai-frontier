The Treasury bureau that collects banks' suspicious activity reports says an intruder got into some of its systems.

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, known as FinCEN, disclosed on Tuesday that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that "resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems." It has taken some systems offline as a precaution. The bureau has not said who was responsible, when the intrusion began, which systems were reached or whether any data was taken.

Banks can still file suspicious activity reports online, though some services may not be immediately available. The bureau's Financial Institutions Hotline, 1-866-556-3974, was not affected.

The same day, FinCEN issued Order FIN-2026-R002, giving certain filers until December 30 to submit reports that would otherwise have been due between November 18 and December 29. The relief covers financial institutions that file Bank Secrecy Act reports in batches, companies filing beneficial ownership reports and people updating a FinCEN identifier. The order describes the delay as a "service disruption" and does not mention the cybersecurity incident.

FinCEN's disclosure came hours after The New York Times reported a breach of an unclassified intelligence system used by the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence — a story covered in Edition No. 014. It is unclear whether the two incidents are related. Social media posts that described the FinCEN incident as an attack on the whole Treasury Department overstated what the bureau said: FinCEN described access to a limited subset of its own systems, not a department-wide compromise.
