Sierra, the enterprise customer-experience company co-founded by former Salesforce co-chief executive Bret Taylor, has raised $1.75 billion in a Series F round, doubling its valuation to $30 billion. The new round comes within six months of the previous one, which valued the company at $15 billion.

The round was led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners, with participation from existing investors including Benchmark, Sequoia, GV, and Tiger Global. Sierra says it serves almost half of the Fortune 50, with clients including BNY, HSBC, Singtel, Wells Fargo, JPMorganChase, Pearson, Next, and Cox Automotive. Late last month, the company said its outcome-based pricing model had helped it double run-rate revenue to $400 million within five months.

Sierra is building a platform for creating customer-service agents and partnering with model developers, and is working on protocol definitions for agents with Meta, Stripe, and Walmart. It plans to use the money to build out its technology stack — layering agents for servicing — and to hire more sales staff.

ElevenLabs is now valued at $22 billion, Decagon at $4.5 billion, Wonderful at $5 billion, and Parloa at $3 billion. At the HumanX conference in Amsterdam in September, Sierra co-founder Clay Bavor argued that despite the belief that AI customer-service companies are replacing people in contact centers, a majority of Sierra’s customers are reassigning roles to existing employees.
