The National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health filed a federal Request for Information on November 20 asking for hard data on how humanoid robots injure workers — the first federal review aimed at building a national research agenda for bipedal machines on the job.

The notice, logged as Federal Register Document 2026-23799 under Docket Numbers CDC-2026-1882 and NIOSH-366, opens a 90-day public comment window that closes February 21, 2027 (one other outlet reports February 22). NIOSH's Center for Occupational Robotics Research is running the effort. Rather than propose binding rules immediately, the agency wants empirical evidence before humanoid robots become common in factories, warehouses, and hospitals.

The request lists eight research priorities, spanning which tasks are actually suited to humanoid execution, how workers' cognitive load changes when a robot walks past them, how to log near-misses and pinch points, and what sensor hardware — proximity beacons, wearable monitors — can keep people safe in shared space. It also covers sensor systems, emergency stop design, hazard surveillance, and performance measurement and validation.

One hazard the agency flags shows why bipedal machines need their own rules. Cutting power to a humanoid robot — the safe move for a stationary industrial arm — can instead cause the machine to fall on nearby workers. OSHA currently has no standalone standard for humanoid robots and relies on general machine-guarding rules plus voluntary ANSI and ISO standards.

The request is for information-gathering only: it does not propose new regulations, requirements, or policies. The feedback NIOSH collects is expected to flow into the OSHA-NIOSH-A3 Robotics Alliance, which would publish hazard alerts and training guidance once the docket closes. That timeline lines up closely with the industry's own rollout schedule: large manufacturers including GM, BMW, and Mercedes-Benz are already running humanoid pilots on live production lines, and several plan to scale deployments through 2027.
