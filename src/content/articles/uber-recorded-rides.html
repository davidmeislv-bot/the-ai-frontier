Uber is piloting a new option in the United States: rides that are recorded by default. The feature, called Record My Ride, uses the front camera of the driver’s phone to record the entire trip, and shows up in the app as “Recorded Rides.”

The pilot is running in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis, and St. Louis, with more locations to come. Uber says it is testing recorded rides specifically for trips booked late at night and for rides involving teenagers. Earlier this year it introduced a live video streaming feature for rides booked for teenagers, and in another test it enabled audio recording for late-night trips in some cities. Uber is also testing a feature that switches recording on when the app detects a ride going off course, or when the vehicle may have been in an accident.

Recording is not mandatory for either side. Drivers can opt out through the Settings panel, and riders can decline permission to record through an in-app notification.
