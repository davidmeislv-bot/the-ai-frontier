The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency gave Payoneer preliminary conditional approval on November 20 to form PAYO Digital Bank, a national trust bank designed to issue a dollar stablecoin — the latest step in a queue of crypto and fintech firms seeking federal bank charters before the new U.S. stablecoin rules take effect on January 18, 2027.

The approval, Corporate Decision No. 1395, lets Payoneer organize the bank but not open it for customer business. PAYO Digital Bank would be wholly owned by Payoneer Global Inc., and its permitted business includes stablecoin issuance, fiduciary custody of fiat currency and its own stablecoin, PAYO-USD, on-ramp and off-ramp services, and conversions among fiat, PAYO-USD, and third-party stablecoins. Payoneer plans to issue PAYO-USD as a fully reserved dollar stablecoin inside a closed-loop banking platform, and Davis Polk, which advised Payoneer, says the bank plans to operate as a permitted payment stablecoin issuer under the GENIUS Act.

The conditions are concrete. The OCC requires the proposed bank to maintain at least $8 million in Tier 1 capital during its first three years of operation, raise that capital within 12 months, and open within 18 months of preliminary approval or risk expiration. It must also hold liquidity covering 180 days of operating expenses. Final approval and authorization to commence business will not be granted until all pre-opening requirements are met. And the bank would be an uninsured national trust bank, not an FDIC-insured retail bank — it takes no deposits from the public in the ordinary sense.

Payoneer is not alone in the queue. MoonPay announced on November 19 that it had applied for a federal charter to establish MoonPay National Trust Bank, headquartered in Miami Beach, Florida, for stablecoin issuance, redemption, reserve management, and digital asset custody. Its filing, dated November 17 and received by the OCC on November 18, says the bank would be a non-depository national trust bank, a Federal Reserve member bank, and would not seek federal deposit insurance. Twelve of its fifteen exhibits are confidential, withheld at the request of its counsel, Sullivan & Cromwell.

The OCC granted conditional approvals last December to Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets, and Paxos. Circle received final approval in July 2026, Ripple in October, and Coinbase's charter became effective earlier this month. Stripe's Bridge, Crypto.com, and World Liberty Financial hold conditional approvals. The expansion has drawn opposition: the Independent Community Bankers of America sued the OCC in October 2026, arguing the regulator exceeded its authority by allowing non-depository national trust banks to conduct activities traditionally associated with commercial banks.

The pattern is now clear enough to name: with the GENIUS Act's January 18, 2027 effective date two months out, the firms that move other people's money in crypto are racing to become banks first — and the OCC is letting them in line, one conditional approval at a time.
