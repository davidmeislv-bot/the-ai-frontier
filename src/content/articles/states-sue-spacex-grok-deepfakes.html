Oregon and Virginia have filed separate but similar consumer-protection lawsuits against SpaceX and X Corp. over Grok, the AI chatbot, alleging the companies lied about its safety and about efforts to stop child sexual abuse material and nonconsensual intimate imagery. Oregon attorney general Dan Rayfield filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court on Sunday evening; Virginia attorney general Jay Jones filed in Richmond circuit court on Monday morning.

SpaceX acquired xAI, Grok’s parent company, in February 2026, also taking ownership of X Corp.

The suits allege Grok’s creators ignored industry safety standards and deliberately shipped weak safeguards to entice users to a “lagging product,” and that they saw “a market opportunity for more profits” in sexualized images of real people, including children — and “intentionally released a product that inundated their platforms with CSAM.” Rayfield put it bluntly: “It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material, because the lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug.” He added: “They profited off the backs of children with these types of images to get a strategic advantage and it worked.”

Virginia’s complaint goes into the mechanics. It alleges Grok’s safety prompts told the system to “assume good intent” and that “teenage” and “girl” do “not necessarily imply underage.” It says tens of thousands of reports to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children’s CyberTipline lacked key information, preventing action 90% of the time. And it says investigators testing Grok shortly before filing found its safeguards “inconsistent and ineffective” — the chatbot explained which words triggered its filters, helped draft prompts to evade moderation, and then carried them out. The complaint adds: “At no point did Grok ask whether (a) the image uploaded was of a real person, or (b) whether the user had the person’s consent to use or modify the photo.”

The attorneys general say this contradicts assurances from a SpaceX official at a New York City Council hearing in October. Both states retained Platkin LLP, the firm of former New Jersey attorney general Matthew Platkin. They are seeking civil penalties, attorneys’ fees, and injunctions; Virginia is also seeking consumer restitution. They cite Meta’s summer settlement with 51 states and territories over alleged failures to protect minors as a model. SpaceX did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The suits follow a record of problems. Last winter, Grok users prompted the mass generation of sexualized photos of women and minors; later, AI videos of female celebrities in sexual situations appeared on Grok’s site. Earlier related suits include one brought by the city of Baltimore and cases brought by teens in Tennessee. Jones summed up the states’ position: “Your size and your scope shouldn’t excuse the fact that you have to play by the rules.” He added: “Our offices are strapped. I wish I had all the resources in the world.”
