Microsoft has put its email business on crisis footing after a day-long outage this month exposed how slowly it responds when its own systems fail.

The company declared an internal "Code Red" — its most severe alert — after a nearly 24-hour failure on November 12 and 13 knocked out Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, its Copilot assistant and other Microsoft 365 services, according to internal memos reviewed by Reuters. The outage hit governments in Australia, Japan and the United States, delayed trading firms and disrupted airline communications.

Jason Henderson, president of Microsoft 365 Core, told staff in a memo on Friday that the outage was the most customer-impacting incident in the history of Exchange Online, and that never before had so many customers been without email for so long. "Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team," he wrote.

The memos describe a response that struggled as much as the systems did. Amanda Silver, a corporate vice president overseeing Microsoft 365 Core, wrote in a memo on Monday that engineers worked through a "fog of war," exchanging more than 11,000 messages across 11 Teams chats while diagnostic data sat scattered across fragmented tracking systems. Charles Lamanna, an executive vice president for Copilot and platform work, told a Code Red review that the failed component had accumulated "roughly ten years' worth of acceptable failures."

Microsoft traced the outage to a Windows scalability defect introduced in July that went undetected for months. At its worst point, more than 80 percent of the gateway servers that connect users to Microsoft 365 stopped responding. A Teams traffic failover added extra load, and maintenance had left nearly 14 percent of gateway capacity offline. The company said the outage was not caused by a cyberattack or by AI agents, and chief executive Satya Nadella publicly apologized.

Under the Code Red, Silver laid out five workstreams covering load shedding, splitting traffic by region and faster recovery. Microsoft set a target of restoring service worldwide within an hour during future major incidents, and a matching one-hour target for deploying urgent security fixes around the world — a recent emergency fix took five to six days to reach worldwide coverage. The company also plans to use AI agents to help engineers read system health data more quickly. Henderson said he would hold daily meetings with the Substrate leadership team, which oversees the engineering group behind Microsoft 365 services, and review progress weekly.
