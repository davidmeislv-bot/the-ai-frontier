Google's $3 model sets off a price war the frontier labs can't ignore

Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 per million input tokens — less than half what OpenAI and Anthropic charge for their top models — and it arrives in a week when nearly every lab cut prices.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 20, 2026 · 2 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The price: Gemini 4 Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens — less than half the $8 input price of OpenAI's and Anthropic's top models.
  • The benchmarks: Neon ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 58.6, but third on the overall Intelligence Index at 61, behind Claude Fable 5.2 at 65 and GPT-6.2 Astra at 62.
  • The week: Alibaba cut average cost per task by roughly 60 percent with Qwen4-Max; Tencent's Hy4 Flash costs $0.07 per million input tokens.
  • The caveat: Benchmark rankings mix company claims and independent indices, and the test sets differ. Alibaba's and Tencent's figures are the companies' own.

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday, and the number that matters is not its benchmark score. It is $3.

That is what Neon costs per million input tokens — less than half the $8 that OpenAI and Anthropic charge for their top models. Neon also costs $15 per million output tokens, and Google says it ranks first on two legal and tax benchmarks and first overall on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, with a score of 58.6. On the same firm's Intelligence Index it scores 61, third overall, behind Claude Fable 5.2 at 65 and GPT-6.2 Astra at 62.

Neon is the fourth Gemini 4 model in seven weeks, and it arrives in the middle of a week in which nearly every lab cut prices. Alibaba released Qwen4-Max on Monday and said it cut average cost per task by roughly 60 percent against its predecessor while using 35 percent fewer tokens. Tencent launched Hy4 Flash the same day at $0.07 per million input tokens — less than one-hundredth of Astra's price — and said that in a blind test of 85 internal users and 259 office tasks, satisfaction came close to Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5. Google's own cheaper model, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, released November 12 at half Neon's price, is claimed by the company to complete tasks two and a half times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash.

The cheapest work of all is no longer writing. Google launched Gemini Decision 1 on November 11, a model that does not write text but picks among predefined options and returns a confidence score — for routing service requests, screening transactions, or checking whether a submission meets policy. It evaluates up to 1,000 questions in a single call in under a quarter of a second, at roughly one-eightieth the price of Astra. Microsoft answered on Tuesday with Microsoft-Decision-1.1, which the company says runs more than twice as fast as its predecessor at the same price, can decline to decide when its confidence is low, and was built on Gemma 4, Google's open model. More than 4,000 customers used the preview.

The week's releases point in two directions at once. OpenAI put frontier cybersecurity capability on general sale with GPT-6.2, which meets the company's Critical threshold for cyber work — the same classification that led it to restrict its earlier GPT-6 Cyber model to vetted security teams on November 5. At the same time, the cost of ordinary AI work keeps falling fast enough that price is ceasing to be the reason not to use it. The question for the labs is no longer who has the best model. It is who can afford to keep selling it this cheaply.
