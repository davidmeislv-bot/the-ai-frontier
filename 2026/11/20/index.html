California subpoenas Nvidia — and the company closes its last legal door to China

Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia with an investigative subpoena on Thursday evening, opening a state probe into whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China — and whether the company misled investors about the risk.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 20, 2026 · 2 min read

Read the full story →

Illustration: Wire Willow. California's subpoena opens a state probe into Nvidia's China ties.

The story in 30 seconds

  • The subpoena: California Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia an investigative subpoena on Thursday evening, November 19, asking whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether the company misled investors.
  • The halt: Interim CEO Colette Kress told analysts Nvidia is “no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China,” and its forecast assumes no China data center compute revenue.
  • The caveat: The subpoena opens an investigation, not a charge. No authority has accused Nvidia or Huang of sending chips to China; the criminal investigation is into Megaspeed, not Huang.
  • The stakes: Licensed Hopper products brought in $2.4 billion from China in the quarter that ended October 25 — about 2 percent of Nvidia's $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue.

Three more things

Anthropic's first day as a public company: up 34 percent

Anthropic's shares opened on the Nasdaq on Thursday at $111.50, about 34 percent above the $83 IPO price set on Wednesday — the market's first verdict on the biggest initial public offering in history. The offering closed on Friday with the underwriters exercising their full option for 147 million additional shares, bringing gross proceeds to about $93.5 billion, with about $91.6 billion going to the company. The separate $26 billion private placement to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC also closed.

Sources: Anthropic, Nov 20 · The Tech Hacker, Nov 20

Payhawk's $280M, Dave's bank charter, and the World Bank's stablecoin test

Payhawk raised a $280 million Series C at a $2 billion valuation, led by Growth Equity at Goldman Sachs Alternatives — the day's biggest fintech round. Dave, the mobile banking app, filed with the OCC and the FDIC for a national bank charter to form Dave Bank, N.A., and crypto payments company MoonPay filed for a charter of its own, for a stablecoin-issuing arm. And the World Bank Group said it will test USDC disbursements with Citi and Circle, while DBS opened direct USDC minting and redemption to institutional clients through a Circle partnership.

Sources: Builders in Fintech, Nov 19 · Law360, Nov 19

151 humanoid robots danced in Shenzhen — and set a world record

One hundred and fifty-one humanoid robots performed a synchronized dance routine in Shenzhen on November 17, setting a Guinness World Record for the most humanoid robots dancing simultaneously within one hour. The event beat its initial target of 110 robots, and adjudicator Xiong Wen confirmed all 151 machines completed the performance. Twenty of ENGINEAI's full-size T800 humanoids — 1.73 meters tall, about 75 kilograms — demonstrated the company's swarm-control capabilities, joined by groups of Unitree G1 and AGIBOT X2 robots. The Shenzhen Longgang District Artificial Intelligence (Robotics) Bureau organized the event ahead of the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18–19. It was a choreographed showcase, not a capability test — but coordinating 151 bipedal machines to move in step is itself the hard part of the humanoid problem.

Sources: Interesting Engineering, Nov 19 · RobotToday, Nov 19
