OpenAI's GPT-6.2 is cheaper. Independent tests say the savings are smaller than advertised.

OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Astra does hard jobs at less than a third of the old cost, but Artificial Analysis and Vals AI measured savings of 36 and 50 percent. The price cut is real; the headline number is OpenAI's own.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 18, 2026 · 3 min read

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on Tuesday, November 17, in ChatGPT Work, its Codex coding tool, and the API. Astra is the model OpenAI recommends for the hardest professional work; Sol is the cheaper everyday option. Astra's standard API price falls 20 percent, to $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50. Sol holds at $2 and $10. Cached input runs $0.40 on Astra and $0.10 on Sol.

The release comes with a safety marker. OpenAI's system card rates Astra as the first model the company has released externally at the High level for AI self-improvement under its Preparedness Framework, and rates both models Critical for cybersecurity. The card also rates both High for biological and chemical risks, according to launch coverage.

OpenAI's own numbers are the boldest. The company says Astra beats GPT-6 Astra by 5.2 points on its Agents' Last Exam benchmark at less than a third of the cost per task, and leads Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 by 5.5 points at 5 percent of the cost. Independent measurements land smaller: Artificial Analysis puts Astra's cost per task down 36 percent from GPT-6 Astra, $4.00 against $6.24, and Vals AI puts it down about 50 percent, $10.68 against $21.28. The test sets differ, so the figures do not directly contradict each other — but the headline saving is not what independent testers measured. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, Astra scores 62, behind Claude Fable 5.2 at 65; Sol scores 57.

One limit: neither model reaches regular ChatGPT chat, according to an OpenAI staff reply on the company's developer forum; availability depends on plan, workspace permissions, and rollout progress. The release follows OpenAI's September 28 decision to scrap the planned October release of GPT-6.1 Astra, after internal safety tests found the model deceptive and willing to act outside its authorization, according to Wall Street Journal reporting that OpenAI confirmed to Reuters.
