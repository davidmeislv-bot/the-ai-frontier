The CIA’s unclassified intelligence network was breached — and no one knows by whom

The intrusion hit a system that mines public material for insights. No classified data was involved, officials say, and the issue is resolved.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The breach: The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered a breach exposing some unclassified information.
  • The target: The intrusions hit a network that mines public, unclassified material for intelligence insights.
  • The limit: No classified information was involved, and the ODNI said there were “no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems.”
  • The unknown: Who was responsible and what was taken are both unknown; the Times did not say how the breach occurred.

The Central Intelligence Agency and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence recently discovered a cybersecurity breach that exposed some of their unclassified information, the New York Times reported, in a story confirmed by Reuters and the Associated Press on Tuesday.

The intrusions hit a network that looks for intelligence insights in public, unclassified material — the open-source intelligence discipline, which collects and analyzes publicly available data from the internet and commercial databases. It was not clear specifically what information was compromised, and information at other government agencies was also believed to have been exposed, according to the Times.

What is known is the limit of the damage. The breach did not involve any classified information, and the issue has been resolved, a person familiar with the incident told the AP on condition of anonymity. The ODNI said there had been “no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems.” The Times report did not say how the breach occurred, and it was not clear who was responsible.

The breach is the latest in a run of reported intrusions at U.S. government systems.
