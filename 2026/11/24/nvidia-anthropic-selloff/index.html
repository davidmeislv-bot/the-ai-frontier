The Nvidia rally gave back a third of its gain in a day — and Anthropic fell below its IPO price

Nvidia dropped 7.2 percent and Anthropic 9.8 percent on Tuesday, as the market’s AI euphoria met profit-taking and export-curb doubts.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • Nvidia: Fell 7.2 percent to $153.19 on Tuesday, erasing about $267 billion — roughly 30 percent of Monday’s gain.
  • Anthropic: Fell 9.8 percent to $79.06, below its $83 IPO price and 29 percent below its $111.50 debut open.
  • The trigger: Chinese state media said Huang’s return was unlikely to ease export curbs; Commerce blacklisted seven companies, including an Alibaba unit.
  • The wider market: The Nasdaq 100 slid 2.7 percent and the S&P 500 fell 1 percent, while the Dow gained 0.4 percent.

The artificial-intelligence trade cracked on two fronts on Tuesday. Anthropic slipped below its $83 IPO price less than a week after its record debut, while Nvidia handed back nearly a third of Monday’s record gain.

Nvidia fell 7.2 percent to $153.19, erasing about $267 billion in market value — roughly 30 percent of Monday’s gain. A day earlier the stock had soared 31.5 percent to close at $165.05, adding nearly $1 trillion in market value on the news of Jensen Huang’s return, in the Nasdaq’s best day since April 2025. Chinese state media said Huang’s return was unlikely to ease U.S. export curbs, and the Commerce Department blacklisted seven companies, including an Alibaba unit, in a probe into alleged transfers of Nvidia chips to China. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF dropped 7.1 percent and the iShares Semiconductor ETF lost 8 percent.

Anthropic fell 9.8 percent to $79.06, 29 percent below the $111.50 open on its November 19 debut. A regulatory filing on Monday showed chief executive Dario Amodei sold more than 3.37 million shares, worth over $280 million, the day after the listing. Wedbush Securities started coverage on Tuesday with an Outperform rating and a $120 price target. Anthropic had already sat out Monday’s rally, closing down 1.4 percent at $87.64 while Nvidia surged.

The selling spread beyond the two names. The Nasdaq 100 slid 2.7 percent to 26,034 and the S&P 500 fell 1 percent to 7,167, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.4 percent to 49,783 as investors rotated into health care, retailers, and blue chips. The technology sector was the worst performer, down 3.4 percent; health care led, up 2.8 percent, helped by a $41 billion takeover lifting drugmakers. The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index jumped to 103.3 in November from 91.9, far above the 92.5 forecast and its highest level since January 2025. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 5 basis points to 4.89 percent. Gold dropped 2.4 percent to $3,564 an ounce, and bitcoin fell 1.9 percent to $60,007.
