Z.ai’s new model reuses the same layers twice and claims the reasoning of a model four times its size

GLM-5.4 is a 750-billion-parameter model built on a Loop Transformer. The company has published no benchmarks to back the claim.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The design: GLM-5.4 has 78 stored layers — a 26-layer prelude, a 28-layer loop, and a 24-layer coda — and the loop runs twice per token, for 106 layer passes.
  • The claim: Z.ai says the roughly 750-billion-parameter model reasons like a model with two to three trillion parameters.
  • The caveat: Z.ai published no model card or benchmark scores; the one independent outing ranked it 7th overall and 1st among open models on KingBench 4.
  • The price: Access is limited to Z.ai products and GLM Coding Plan subscribers at $18, $80, or $168 a month.

Chinese lab Z.ai released a new flagship model on Monday that takes an unusual approach to getting smarter: instead of adding more parameters, it runs the same layers twice.

GLM-5.4 is built on what Z.ai calls a Loop Transformer. According to The Decoder’s account of the design, the model has 78 stored layers — a 26-layer prelude, a 28-layer loop, and a 24-layer coda — and the 28 loop layers run twice for every token, producing 106 layer passes without adding 28 more layers of unique weights. The model is built on the roughly 750-billion-parameter GLM-5 base, a mixture-of-experts design; TechTimes reports 744 billion total parameters with 40 billion active per token. Z.ai says the design delivers reasoning comparable to models with two to three trillion parameters.

The model adds vision, taking video, image, text, and file input, with a one-million-token context window and up to 128,000 tokens of output. Z.ai says throughput reaches up to 200 tokens per second off-peak and holds at 70 to 80 tokens per second under load. Those figures are the company’s claims.

Access is limited at launch to Z.ai’s products and GLM Coding Plan subscribers — $18 a month for Lite, $80 for Pro, $168 for Max — through ZCode and Z.ai Chat. API access will follow a safety evaluation, and open weights are promised after safety hardening, with no date for either.

The 2–3 trillion equivalence claim has not been independently verified. Z.ai published no model card and no benchmark scores for GLM-5.4, and as of November 23 there was no Hugging Face repository or entry in the developer documentation. The model has had one independent outing: review site AICodeKing ranked it 7th overall and 1st among open models on its KingBench 4 coding suite, though it took 431 minutes to finish against Gemini 4 Neon’s 109. Its strongest published evidence is an ablation study on a separate 30-billion-parameter model, not the 750-billion-parameter flagship, and Z.ai says benchmark scores will come later. The company itself discloses one drawback: the model can get caught in overly long reasoning loops, though it says the extra self-correction still lifts accuracy.
