Uber’s Lucid robotaxis are now driving themselves around the Bay Area — no one behind the wheel

The three companies started fully driverless testing on public roads. The public launch is planned for the first quarter of 2027.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The milestone: Uber, Lucid, and Nuro have started fully driverless testing of their robotaxis on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area.
  • The vehicles: Modified Lucid Gravity SUVs with cameras, LIDAR, and radar, running Nuro’s Driver autonomous driving system.
  • The rollout: Testing starts in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos, and Sunnyvale, with employee rides soon and a public launch planned for Q1 2027.
  • The scale: Uber has committed to buying 35,000 of the robotaxis; the fleet already numbers 100 vehicles.

The Lucid-built robotaxis Uber showed off at CES earlier this year are almost ready to carry passengers. Uber, Lucid, and Nuro — the company behind the autonomous driving system — have started fully driverless testing on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area, and will soon give their own employees rides in the vehicles ahead of a public launch planned for the first quarter of 2027.

The program itself is not new. The companies announced the global robotaxi effort in July 2025 and have been testing the modified Lucid Gravity vehicles with a driver behind the wheel since December last year. The step to driverless testing follows a California DMV Driverless Testing Permit granted in May, which allows testing on public roads in the state without a driver behind the wheel.

The vehicles carry high-resolution cameras, LIDAR sensors, and radar, and run on Nuro’s Driver end-to-end autonomous driving system. Testing starts on roads in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos, and Sunnyvale, expanding to more Bay Area cities next year. Lucid said its robotaxi fleet already numbers 100 vehicles, though it is unclear whether all of them will be part of the initial rollout.

Uber has committed to buying 35,000 of the Lucid-Nuro robotaxis and plans to offer them around the world. Passengers will summon the rides through the Uber app. Uber will design the in-cabin experience and provide support and rider controls once the vehicles join its network.
