SpaceX and Tesla named the five directors who will negotiate their merger — with a year-end deadline

The FT reports the boards want a deal by December 31, though people familiar with the talks say an agreement isn’t close.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The negotiators: Tesla’s Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson, and Jack Hartung; SpaceX’s Roelof Botha and Randy Glein — advised by Evercore, Morgan Stanley, and Goldman Sachs.
  • The deal: An all-stock merger worth about $3.5 trillion together, by far the largest ever if it happens.
  • The deadline: The boards want a signed deal by December 31, days before a new Congress is seated on January 3.
  • The catch: Musk is excluded from active negotiations; any deal needs more than 50 percent of all outstanding Tesla votes.

Elon Musk’s two companies have named the five directors who will negotiate a possible merger of SpaceX and Tesla, the Financial Times reported on Tuesday — and the boards are working toward a year-end deadline, even though people familiar with the talks say an agreement is not close.

Tesla’s side of the table is board chair Robyn Denholm, Kathleen Wilson-Thompson, and Jack Hartung, advised by Evercore. SpaceX is represented by Roelof Botha and Randy Glein, with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs as advisers. The committees are fine-tuning financial models that will form the basis of their negotiating positions in an all-stock merger, and bankers are studying where the businesses of a combined company would overlap. The deal under discussion would combine the two companies — worth about $3.5 trillion together, by far the largest merger ever if it happens.

Musk himself is not formally in the room. The FT says Texas governance rules keep him out of active negotiations, though both committees can consult senior executives. He confirmed on SpaceX’s third-quarter earnings call on October 20 that both boards had formed special committees of independent directors at shareholders’ request, that no terms had been agreed, and that he cannot take part because he is conflicted. He holds more than 50 percent of SpaceX’s voting power.

The deadline adds pressure. A signed deal by December 31 would land days before a new Congress is seated on January 3, in which Democrats have promised to scrutinize Musk’s companies. Any agreement would then go to a separate extraordinary Tesla shareholder meeting, needing more than 50 percent of all outstanding votes — not just of votes cast. There is a wrinkle in who is negotiating: Denholm and Wilson-Thompson were the two-person special committee that designed Musk’s $1 trillion pay package, which has change-in-control terms that drop its operational milestones and vest earned shares at the deal price — so the committee that wrote it is now negotiating the deal that could trigger it.

Bloomberg reported on November 12 that a deal could be announced as early as December. The market took the latest report coolly on Tuesday: SpaceX shares fell 5.4 percent to $126.21 and Tesla slipped 0.6 percent to $383.08.
