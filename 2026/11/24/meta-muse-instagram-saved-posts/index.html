Meta is putting Muse inside Instagram — starting with your saved posts

The assistant will prompt users to organize saves into collections and answer in DMs. Meta AI stays as a separate chatbot.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The move: Meta is integrating Muse into Instagram, starting by prompting users to organize saved posts into collections.
  • In DMs: Muse gets its own presence in Instagram DMs, where users can ask it to research trips, shop, or check other threads.
  • The catch: Instagram’s separate Meta AI chatbot is not going away; Muse in DMs is more limited than the Muse app or website.
  • The audience: US users with connected Instagram and Muse accounts see the prompts first; Muse passed 10 million users last month.

Meta is bringing its personal agent Muse directly into Instagram, and the first thing it will do is sort through the posts people have saved for years and never organized.

The company is integrating Muse into the app and will begin prompting people to use the agent when they save posts, suggesting it organize saved content into collections such as recipe videos or travel clips. Muse is also getting its own presence in Instagram DMs, so users can start chats with the assistant based on the content they are seeing — sending it travel posts to research for a trip, asking it to shop for specific items, or having it check on other DM threads. The features are available to people who have connected their Instagram and Muse accounts, and users in the United States will be the first to see prominent pop-ups suggesting they use Muse to organize their saves.

It is a familiar move from Meta’s growth playbook. When the company was growing Threads, it pushed popular posts from the app to the top of Facebook and Instagram feeds; Threads now has more than 500 million users. Meta is likely hoping the in-app nudges will draw more Instagram users to Muse, which passed 10 million users last month. Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg has said he wants Muse to one day reach billions of people and has predicted it could eventually generate more revenue for the company than its existing apps.

The push could also confuse users. Instagram still has a separate Meta AI chatbot that users can DM with, meant as a generic AI that answers questions. That bot is not going away, according to an Instagram spokesperson. Muse in DMs is meant to be more of an assistant-like experience — but it will still be more limited than the actual Muse app or website. Users can send it to-dos or have it track Instagram-specific tasks, though they will probably still need the Muse app or website to see what it does with their Instagram messages.
