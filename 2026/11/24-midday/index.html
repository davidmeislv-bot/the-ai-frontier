Huang told Nvidia staff Trump didn’t get him rehired — and that he’s in therapy

At his first all-hands since returning, the reinstated chief apologized, denied politics played a role, and said he had affairs beyond the two already reported.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 24, 2026 · 3 min read

Read the full story →

Illustration: Wire Willow. Huang addressed Nvidia employees at his first all-hands meeting since returning as chief executive.

The story in 30 seconds

  • The meeting: At his first all-hands since returning, Huang apologized for the affairs that led to his November 12 resignation and said he attends therapy for 90 minutes a day.
  • The denial: Huang told staff it was “completely untrue” that President Trump got him rehired, though he thanked the administration for intelligence access that helped the board assess the risks.
  • The oversight: His direct reports will now send reviews of his behavior to the board, and he will submit to a performance review as often as once a month.
  • What’s next: He faces a closed-door House interview on December 1 and Senate Banking testimony on December 3.

New York’s top court says social media companies aren’t liable for the Buffalo shooting

New York’s highest court ruled on Monday that Google, Meta, Reddit, and Snap should not be held liable over the 2022 mass shooting at a Buffalo grocery store, in which 10 Black people were killed. In a 4-3 decision, the Court of Appeals let stand a lower court ruling that found Alphabet’s Google and YouTube, Meta’s Instagram, Reddit, and Snap’s Snapchat were immune under Section 230, the federal law that protects online platforms from liability over user content. The majority said survivors and victims’ relatives failed to show that shooter Payton Gendron’s attack on May 14, 2022, at Tops Friendly Markets was a “natural and foreseeable consequence” of how the companies designed and ran their platforms. Dissenting, Judge Jenny Rivera said juries should decide whether the prevalence of mass shootings put the companies on notice, writing that no law “requires that we slam the courthouse door on plaintiffs’ claims.” The ruling also upheld dismissal of claims against other messaging and social media apps and against Amazon, which owns Twitch, the service Gendron used to livestream the attack.

Sources: Reuters · Associated Press · The Verge

TikTok went down for tens of thousands of users on Tuesday afternoon

TikTok went down on Tuesday afternoon, with videos, search, favorites, and profiles failing to load across iOS, Android, and the web. Downdetector showed about 25,000 to 30,000 user reports at the peak, against a baseline of about 11 — a spike more than 2,000 times the usual level. Reports began surging around midday Pacific time. By about 12:40 p.m. PT, reports had fallen to roughly 2,400, suggesting the situation was easing, though that does not mean the outage was resolved. TikTok has not publicly explained the issue or given an estimated time for restoring normal service, and there is no confirmed explanation for what caused it.

Sources: The Verge · Neowin · Windows Report
