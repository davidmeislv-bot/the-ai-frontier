Anthropic goes public at $83 a share in a record-setting $81.3 billion IPO

The maker of Claude raised more in its initial offering than any company before it, and it has nearly $900 billion in computing commitments to pay for.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The price: $83 a share, above the $70–$78 marketed range.
  • The raise: 980 million shares sold for $81.3 billion — the largest initial IPO raise on record. SpaceX's total later reached $86.2 billion after its overallotment.
  • The valuation: Roughly $1.4 trillion to $1.5 trillion fully diluted, trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT — and about $2 trillion at Thursday's open.
  • The catch: About $900 billion in future compute commitments, and a $104 billion net loss in the first nine months — mostly an accounting charge on convertible notes, not cash.

Anthropic starts trading on the Nasdaq today after raising more in its initial share sale than any company in history.

The company behind the Claude AI chatbot priced its shares at $83 on Wednesday evening, above the $70 to $78 range it had marketed to investors, and sold 980 million Class A shares — more than the 825 million originally planned. About 956 million of those shares were sold by Anthropic itself; existing shareholders sold the rest. The offering raised $81.3 billion, passing the $75 billion SpaceX raised when it priced its IPO in June. SpaceX's total later reached $86.2 billion after its underwriters bought additional shares under their overallotment option, so whether Anthropic stays ahead on that measure depends on what its own underwriters do.

At $83 a share, Anthropic is valued at roughly $1.4 trillion to $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, depending on the share count used, putting it among the 15 largest publicly traded companies in the world. It will trade on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. The offering is scheduled to close on Friday, November 20.

The deal comes with a second, separate piece: a $26 billion private placement in which AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC bought shares at the IPO price. Counting only the shares it sold itself, Anthropic is bringing in about $105 billion from the public offering and the placement combined.

The money has a specific destination. Anthropic has committed roughly $900 billion to future computing capacity — the data centers and chips it needs to train its models and serve its users — and it is going public in large part to finance those commitments. The company spent nearly $9 billion on infrastructure in the first half of 2026 alone.

The growth behind the valuation is real, and so is the spending. Anthropic's revenue reached $16.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026, bringing sales for the first nine months of the year to $33.24 billion, up from $2.55 billion in the same period of 2025. Its annualized revenue run rate reached about $81 billion by October, and the number of customers spending at least $100,000 a year grew to around 11,900.

The company is not yet profitable. It recorded a $1.26 billion operating loss in the first nine months of 2026, an improvement from a $5.22 billion loss a year earlier. Its net loss for the same period was far larger, about $104 billion, mostly because of changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities — an accounting charge, not cash spent.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley led the underwriting syndicate alongside 20 other financial institutions. Up to 20 percent of the offering was reserved for retail investors.

The debut lands in a choppy market. The tech-heavy Nasdaq is down more than 10 percent from its record high six weeks ago, in a correction that accelerated after the midterm elections on November 3 and the scandal that forced out Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, according to Le Monde. None of that slowed demand for Anthropic's shares: the deal was upsized in both price and size.

Trading began on Thursday, and the market's first answer was emphatic: ANT opened at $111.50, 34 percent above the $83 IPO price, putting Anthropic's diluted market value at roughly $2 trillion. The $83 price is the outcome of the institutional bookbuilding process, not necessarily the price at which the public can buy. If ANT trades consistently above $83 after the opening session, investors will be valuing Anthropic above the level implied by the IPO. If it trades below, the question will be whether a company with nearly $900 billion in computing commitments can grow into a valuation that already assumes it will.
