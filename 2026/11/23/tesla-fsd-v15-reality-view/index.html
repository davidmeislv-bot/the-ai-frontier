Tesla’s new self-driving software rebuilds the view from the driver’s seat

Full Self-Driving v15 adds a photorealistic overhead view and claims a big jump in safety — but only newer cars get it, and regulators are still watching.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The update: Full Self-Driving v15 (build 2026.38.6.11) rebuilds the end-to-end driving network and began rolling out to early-access owners on November 21.
  • Reality View: A real-time, AI-generated overhead visualization that renders buildings, sidewalks, people, and the car’s planned path from a virtual camera above the vehicle.
  • The claim: Tesla says the update delivers a 25-fold improvement in handling safety-critical cases. That figure is Tesla’s claim and has not been independently verified.
  • The catch: Version 15 is limited to cars with the newer AI4 hardware; HW3 cars will not receive it. The system is still supervised, costs $99 a month, and the NHTSA is investigating about 2.9 million FSD-equipped vehicles.

Tesla has begun rolling out a ground-up overhaul of its Full Self-Driving software, version 15, to early-access owners. The update rebuilds the end-to-end driving network — build 2026.38.6.11, or FSD v15.0.1 — and changes what the driver sees on screen.

The centerpiece is “Tesla Reality View,” a real-time, AI-generated photorealistic overhead visualization that renders buildings, sidewalks, people, and the car’s planned path from a virtual camera above the vehicle. It replaces the hard-coded 3D model signals the old display used. Tesla vice president Ashok Elluswamy says the new view uses less computing power than the system it replaces.

Tesla claims the update delivers a 25-fold improvement in handling safety-critical cases, measured by the rate of critical interventions, through a new vision encoder shared with Robotaxi and Optimus and trained on millions of internet videos. That figure is Tesla’s claim and has not been independently verified. The update also adds a Bike Rack Mode, automatic slowdowns near speed cameras, and improved hazard avoidance.

Not every Tesla gets it. Version 15 is limited to cars with the newer AI4 hardware — the first builds have been spotted on refreshed Model Ys in the United States — and cars with the older HW3 hardware will not receive it. The system is still supervised: the driver must keep watching the road. It costs $99 a month in the United States. Elon Musk said on X that “FSD V15 goes to wide release next month,” with the wider rollout expected in December as part of the holiday update.

The release lands while the regulator is still looking. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is investigating roughly 2.9 million vehicles equipped with Full Self-Driving over reports of dangerous behavior.
