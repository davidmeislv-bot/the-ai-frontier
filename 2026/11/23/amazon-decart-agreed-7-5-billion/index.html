Amazon is buying an AI startup’s tech for $7.5 billion to take on Nvidia’s chips

Decart’s software squeezes more work out of every chip — and Amazon wants it to help sell its own Trainium processors to customers.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The deal: Amazon agreed to acquire the technology of Israeli startup Decart for $7.5 billion, according to Bloomberg, along with its founding team and AI researchers.
  • The prize: Decart’s Optimization Stack software helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon, and Google hardware.
  • The motive: Amazon has used its Trainium chips only in its own data centers; it is now preparing to sell them to customers as a rival to Nvidia.
  • The voice: “Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Amazon’s Peter DeSantis.

Amazon has agreed to acquire the technology of Israeli startup Decart, paying $7.5 billion, according to Bloomberg. The deal brings Amazon the software behind Decart’s Optimization Stack, along with the company’s founding team and its AI researchers and engineers, Amazon said in a blog post.

Decart is best known for interactive, real-time world models — most recently Oasis 3 — and AI video-generation tools. The part Amazon wants is less visible: software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon, and Google hardware.

“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president for foundational AI models, custom silicon, and quantum computing. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.”

The purchase fits a larger move. Amazon has so far used its Trainium chips only in its own data centers. It is now preparing to sell them to customers as a rival to Nvidia, whose chips have been the standard for AI work. Decart’s technology could make it easier for customers to run their workloads on Amazon’s chips instead.

Decart and Amazon did not immediately return requests for comment.
