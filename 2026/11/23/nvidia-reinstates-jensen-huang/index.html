Nvidia brings Jensen Huang back as CEO, 11 days after he resigned

The board voted unanimously to reinstate its founder, reversing a decision it had just defended on the earnings call — and the stock jumped more than 30%.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 2 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The return: Nvidia’s board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, eleven days after his November 12 resignation. The return took effect immediately, before Monday’s market open.
  • The reversal: Days earlier, board chair Harvey Jones said on the earnings call that the board had “unanimously accepted” the resignation and “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”
  • The market: Shares jumped about 28% in premarket trading and opened 33% higher, at $167.04, erasing most of a slide that had taken the stock about a third lower in two weeks.
  • What’s next: Huang testifies before Congress on December 1, and has agreed to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, with December 3 requested. The board’s committee is still investigating reports of a foreign intelligence effort.

Nvidia’s board has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, eleven days after he resigned. The vote was unanimous, and the return took effect immediately, before the market opened on Monday.

Huang stepped down on November 12 after admitting at least two extramarital affairs — one with Alice Huang, a tech executive whom U.S. officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence, and another with a subordinate. Beijing denies the intelligence allegation. The board said it reached its decision after “a series of discussions and meetings with Mr. Huang,” and that it was “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA.” It added that it had “received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”

The reversal is sharp. Just days earlier, on the company’s third-quarter earnings call, board chair Harvey Jones said the board had “unanimously accepted” Huang’s resignation and “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Nvidia had retained the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent replacement. The board met on Sunday night to vote, according to Semafor. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who served as interim chief executive, remains chief financial officer.

Jones now says the board believes the return is “in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” and that it has “every confidence in Jensen.” He remains chair.

Pressure for the return had built all weekend. A petition for Huang’s reinstatement drew more than 180,000 signatures, including Elon Musk, Replit chief executive Amjad Masad, Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan. President Donald Trump posted on Sunday night that he hoped the board would rehire Huang, and on Monday called the decision “the correct and proper decision,” adding that the U.S. Attorney General has no evidence Huang engaged in unlawful conduct and that the government has no reason to believe his return poses a national security risk.

Huang said he takes the job back “with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” and that he is “honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution.” He added: “I can’t wait to get back to work.”

Investors welcomed the news. Nvidia shares jumped about 28% in premarket trading on Monday and opened 33% higher, at $167.04. The rebound erased most of the slide since his resignation: shares had closed Friday at $125.53, about a third below where they stood two weeks earlier.

The episode is not fully closed. The board’s independent-director committee is still investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and Huang is set to testify before Congress on December 1 — before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and the House Intelligence Committee — apparently behind closed doors, with his remarks transcribed. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is set to testify the following day. Huang has also agreed to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee; its chair, Tim Scott, has asked for December 3, though the date is not yet fixed.
