Anthropic’s $900 billion compute bill could land it a junk-debt rating, bond managers say

The AI lab has about $70 billion in commitments due within a year, and the rating agencies are expected to grade its debt below investment grade.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The bill: Anthropic’s amended SEC filing puts aggregate purchase and lease commitments at $895.4 billion to $900.9 billion, almost all for computing power and infrastructure.
  • The rating: Kroll already rates Anthropic BB+, and bond-fund managers expect Moody’s, S&P, and Fitch to place the debt in the BB range — one or two notches below investment grade.
  • The cash: Anthropic held $95.6 billion in cash at September 30, before the IPO money arrived; post-IPO estimates range from about $200 billion to about $250 billion.
  • The shares: Anthropic priced at $83 on November 18, opened at $111.50 on November 19, and fell 8.5 percent on Friday to $88.91, leaving its market value at about $1.18 trillion.

Anthropic has committed about $900 billion to computing power and data centers, and bond-fund managers expect the credit-rating firms to grade its debt as junk, according to a report published on Monday.

The company’s amended filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission puts aggregate purchase and lease commitments at $895.4 billion to $900.9 billion, almost all tied to computing power and infrastructure. About $447.5 billion falls due by the end of 2030, and about $70.7 billion is payable within 12 months of September 30. Kroll Bond Rating Agency already rates Anthropic BB+, and bond-fund managers expect Moody’s, S&P, and Fitch to place the debt in the BB range — one or two notches below investment grade. That would make it by far the most valuable high-yield company in the United States.

The warning lands days after the largest public listing on record. Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on November 18, opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq on November 19, closed its first day at $97.17, and fell 8.5 percent on Friday to $88.91, leaving its market value at about $1.18 trillion. The IPO itself raised about $81 billion; including the underwriters’ extra shares the total was $93.5 billion, and gross proceeds reached $119.5 billion counting a $26 billion concurrent private placement from Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC.

At September 30, Anthropic held $95.6 billion in cash, before the IPO money arrived. Estimates of its cash after the listing vary — Wired put it at about $200 billion, the Motley Fool at about $250 billion in cash and short-term investments — enough to cover the near-term bill several times over, but the longer commitments are the question. Broadcom has agreed to absorb losses under a $63 billion equipment-financing deal if Anthropic defaults.

The company’s revenue is growing fast — about $17 billion in the third quarter — but so are its costs. The nine months ended September 30 produced a net loss of about $104 billion, primarily due to accounting changes related to convertible notes, and investor Kevin O’Leary warned on November 17 that Anthropic “may not make money until 2030.”
