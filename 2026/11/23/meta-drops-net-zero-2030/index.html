Meta drops its 2030 net-zero deadline as AI data centers push emissions up

The company’s new sustainability report says the goal is under review with no new date — after emissions more than doubled in four years.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 23, 2026 · 2 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The move: Meta’s 2026 sustainability report says it is re-evaluating its 2030 net-zero target, set in 2020, and has offered no new date.
  • The numbers: Emissions have more than doubled in four years; data-center emissions rose more than 12 times between 2024 and 2025.
  • The driver: A multibillion-dollar bet on natural gas at the Hyperion data center in Louisiana, which is not yet online.
  • The contrast: Amazon, Google, and Microsoft have so far kept their own published sustainability targets.

Meta is walking away from the deadline on its net-zero emissions goal. The company’s 2026 sustainability report says it is re-evaluating the 2030 target — the goal it set in 2020 to reach net zero across its entire value chain — and it has not offered a new date.

“We are re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones — including the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal and our near-term intensity targets — so our approach reflects current operating realities while keeping progress transparent year over year,” wrote Rachel Peterson, Meta’s vice president of data centers, and Blair Swedeen, its global head of net zero and sustainability, in the report’s introduction.

A Meta spokesperson told TechCrunch the company remains committed to net zero but is “re-evaluating the 2030 timeline and interim milestone,” and will keep investing in emissions reductions and reporting progress annually.

The numbers behind the shift are stark. Meta’s greenhouse gas emissions have more than doubled in the last four years. Between 2024 and 2025, its data-center emissions increased more than 12 times. Net emissions rose 45% in 2025 to roughly 11.8 million metric tons of carbon dioxide equivalent, according to the report. The company’s multibillion-dollar bet on natural gas at its Hyperion data center in Louisiana has driven much of the increase, and Hyperion is not online yet.

That bet on gas also led Meta to leave RE100, a corporate renewable-energy initiative, in July. The company had belonged to the group for a decade; the two split after RE100 began requiring more rigorous reporting toward renewable-energy targets.

“When we set our net zero emissions goal, we anticipated steady growth, falling clean energy costs, and changes in the broader system that would enable progress at the pace we envisioned,” Peterson and Swedeen wrote. “The reality has been far more complex. Demand for AI infrastructure has grown faster than almost anyone projected, arriving on a timeline measured in quarters rather than decades, and meeting it has meant building at a pace and scale the industry has never attempted.”

Meta is betting that the buildout will pay off: the company is spending more than $200 billion on data centers, according to Bloomberg. Its peers — Amazon, Google, and Microsoft — have so far kept their own published sustainability targets despite similar AI-driven growth, a contrast TechCrunch argues could blunt competitive pressure across the industry if Meta’s retreat goes unchallenged.
