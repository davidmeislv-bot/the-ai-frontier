Meta is "re-evaluating" its 2030 net-zero goal as AI data centers push its emissions up nearly 40%

The company's carbon footprint jumped from 15.6 million to more than 21.7 million metric tonnes in a year — the biggest percentage rise of any Big Tech giant.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 21, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The rethink: Meta's 2026 sustainability report, published Nov 19, says the company is 're-evaluating' its 2030 net-zero goal and near-term targets.
  • The numbers: Total emissions rose from 15.6 million to more than 21.7 million metric tonnes in a year — nearly 40% — and net emissions rose 45%, to more than 11.8 million tonnes.
  • The comparison: Microsoft's footprint rose 25%, Google's 18% and Amazon's 16%; all three have stuck by their original goals so far.
  • The water: Facility water use fell to 790 million gallons, but use in water-stressed areas rose to 246 million, and power-generation water use topped 26 billion gallons.

Meta says it is "re-evaluating" its goal of reaching net-zero emissions by 2030, after its carbon footprint surged by nearly 40 percent in a single year — the biggest percentage rise reported by any of the major American tech companies.

The admission comes in Meta's 2026 sustainability report, published November 19. In the report's introduction, Rachel Peterson, the company's vice president of data centers, and Blair Swedeen, its global head of net zero and sustainability, wrote that "our long-term ambitions haven't changed, but our operating environment has" — because "demand for AI infrastructure has grown faster than almost anyone projected."

"We are re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones — including the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal and our near-term intensity targets — so our approach reflects current operating realities while keeping progress transparent year over year," they wrote.

The numbers behind the rethink are stark. Meta's total emissions rose from 15.6 million metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent in 2024 to more than 21.7 million in 2025, a footprint roughly comparable to the entire state of Idaho. Net emissions, after renewable energy and carbon removal, rose from more than 8.1 million metric tonnes to more than 11.8 million — a 45 percent increase.

Meta is not alone in the trend, but it is the outlier in scale. Microsoft's footprint rose 25 percent, Google's 18 percent and Amazon's 16 percent over the same period. Amazon, Google and Microsoft have all stuck by their original sustainability goals so far — Meta is the first of the four to publicly reconsider its timeline.

Water tells a mixed story. Meta cut water use at its own facilities from 825 million gallons in 2024 to 790 million in 2025, but its consumption in water-stressed areas rose from 198 million to 246 million gallons, and the water consumed generating the electricity it buys jumped from 19 billion to more than 26 billion gallons. Meta is the only hyperscaler that reports that indirect water use at all, according to Data Center Dynamics.
