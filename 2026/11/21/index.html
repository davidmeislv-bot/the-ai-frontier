Lead · AI

An Anthropic mathematician says Claude helped him prove the Riemann Hypothesis. The proof is 250,000 pages long.

Levent Alpöge says he worked with Claude for six weeks to produce a proof of the 167-year-old problem. One Oxford mathematician says he's '98 or 99 percent certain' it's right — but nobody has verified it yet.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 21, 2026 · 1 min read

Read the full story →

Illustration: Wire Willow. A 250,000-page proof, written with an AI's help.

The story in 30 seconds

  • The claim: Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he worked with Claude for six weeks to build a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, written up in Lean and running to more than 250,000 pages.
  • The prize: The Clay Mathematics Institute offers $1 million for the first correct solution of the 167-year-old problem, proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859.
  • The caveat: The proof has not been verified, and checking it will take time. Oxford mathematician James Maynard says he is '98 or 99 percent certain' it is accurate — his assessment, not a confirmation.
  • The question: It is unclear whether Claude was essential to the breakthrough, or a powerful tool in a mathematician's hands.

Three more things

WIRED: the Nvidia board's problem with Huang was his 'lack of candor'

WIRED reports, citing sources, that the Nvidia board's main issue ahead of Jensen Huang's resignation was the former chief executive's 'lack of candor' — amid allegations of multiple affairs, including with a direct report and an alleged Chinese intelligence asset. The board said on November 17 it 'does not intend' to reinstate Huang as CEO.

Source: WIRED, Nov 20

An AI debt-collection service exposed 6.6 million accounts and 315,000 call recordings

Two serious vulnerabilities in CreditNirvana, an AI debt-collection platform, would have let attackers access 6.6 million accounts and 315,000 call recordings — along with emails, texts and credit reports. The bugs were patched in June and disclosed in October.

Source: WIRED, Nov 18

After a week of talks, the UN agrees to two more years of talking about 'killer robots'

After a week of negotiations in Geneva that ran until 4:30 a.m. on Saturday, 128 countries agreed to spend two years 'elaborating' on a possible accord on lethal autonomous weapons — not 'negotiating' one, a word several countries rejected. About 100 countries had pushed for swift, legally binding rules; the United States, Russia and Israel opposed them. The International Committee of the Red Cross, which wanted binding rules within a year, said it regretted the outcome, and the Stop Killer Robots coalition called the mandate 'watered down.'

Sources: Reuters, Nov 21 · AP, Nov 21
