Signal is blocking screenshots on your phone, because your operating system's AI can read them

The messaging app is extending its screenshot block from Windows to Android and iOS. Its president calls OS-level AI agents an 'existential threat' — and admits the fix is incomplete.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 21, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The change: Signal is blocking screenshots in its Android and iOS apps, after already doing so on Windows, because OS-level AI agents can scan what is on screen.
  • The reason: President Meredith Whittaker calls operating systems that integrate agentic AI an 'existential threat' to private messaging.
  • The limit: Users can turn the block off, which Whittaker says would 'break our privacy guarantees.'
  • The gap: Whittaker admits the block is 'incomplete' — it does not stop sneakier ways AI agents could reach app data.

Signal is blocking screenshots inside its app on Android and iPhone, because the AI agents built into modern operating systems can read whatever is on your screen.

Signal's president, Meredith Whittaker, announced the change on Friday, calling operating systems that integrate agentic AI an "existential threat" to private messaging. By giving AI agents the ability to "scan" the information on users' screens, she wrote, Windows, Android and macOS/iOS "are undermining Signal's ability to independently guarantee privacy."

"Now, in service of AI agentic capabilities, your sensitive data is being captured, stored, and made accessible to whatever local database or remote server an OS-level AI agent is using at that moment," Whittaker wrote. "All of this is outside of Signal's control once the data has been captured, and imperils the privacy of all the participants in a conversation."

Signal already blocked screenshots in its Windows app by default. The new restriction brings Android and iOS into line — but users can turn it off, which Whittaker said would "break our privacy guarantees."

Whittaker was candid about the limits. The screenshot block is an "incomplete" solution, she said, because it does not "address other more sneaky ways AI agents could potentially access application data." The deeper problem is structural: Signal can control its own app, but not the operating system around it — and the operating systems are the ones building the agents.
