Players caught AI-generated songs hiding in a hit game's update — its creator says he never knew

The Binding of Isaac's new expansion included music made with an AI tool, with no disclosure on Steam. The files gave it away, the reviews tanked, and the studio pulled the tracks within days.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 22, 2026 · 2 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The discovery: Players found AI-generated songs in The Binding of Isaac's Repentance+ console expansion, launched November 19; the files carried Suno metadata and no disclosure.
  • The creator: Edmund McMillen said he had no idea the music was generated and that he was looking into how it happened and getting it removed.
  • The fix: Publisher Nicalis pushed a patch listing the "removal of generated music files" first; it is live on PC and has been sent to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony.
  • The fallout: The expansion's recent Steam reviews fell to "Overwhelmingly Negative," with about 8 percent positive.

A beloved indie game's big console launch came with a soundtrack nobody admitted to making. The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ arrived on PlayStation 5, Xbox, and Nintendo Switch 2 on November 19, with a special Grand Theft Auto-themed run timed to the release of GTA 6. Within a day, players worked out that some of the run's music was generated by artificial intelligence — and that the game had never said so.

The evidence was in the files. A Reddit user going by ShweetsStuff pulled the run's audio out of the game and found metadata naming Suno, an AI music tool, with links to the songs' pages on Suno's site. Several audio files were involved, and at least one was dated October 30, less than three weeks before the update shipped. The run itself was credited to a modder known as Mr. Headcrab, who the original poster said opposes AI-generated work and had no idea the music was machine-made.

The game's creator, Edmund McMillen, said he was blindsided. "Woke up to a shit show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned," he wrote on X on November 21. Within hours, the publisher Nicalis pushed out a patch whose notes listed the "removal of generated music files" first, alongside a fix for a broken item combination. The patch is live on PC and has been sent to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony for the console versions. Nicalis has not said how the tracks got into the build.

Players had already delivered their verdict. The expansion's recent Steam reviews fell to "Overwhelmingly Negative," with only about 8 percent positive, after days of review-bombing. The anger was not only about the music. Valve requires developers to say on their Steam pages when AI was used in making a game, and Repentance+ carried no such disclosure. The same update was also blamed for breaking mods and causing crashes.

The episode is a small case with a large lesson. AI-generated material reached a shipped game without the knowledge of the person whose name is on it, and it was caught not by any disclosure or review process but by players reading file metadata. The fix was fast — two days from launch to patch — but the reviews, and the questions about who put the songs there, are still open.
