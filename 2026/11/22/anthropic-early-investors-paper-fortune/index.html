The early believers who turned Anthropic into a paper fortune

Notable Capital, Singapore's GIC, and Baillie Gifford piled into the AI lab at valuations that once looked absurd — and the IPO made them look prescient.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 22, 2026 · 2 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The bet: Notable Capital put about 10 percent of the US portion of its $1 billion-plus fund into Anthropic's Series E at a $61.5 billion valuation.
  • The payoff: Anthropic's IPO priced at $83 a share, raising $81.3 billion at a valuation of about $1.5 trillion fully diluted; Notable's stake is up about 15 times on paper.
  • The others: Singapore's GIC put in more than $4.5 billion across three rounds for about a 1 percent stake; Baillie Gifford put in $1.5 billion across three rounds.
  • The caveat: The gains are paper gains on an illiquid stake; the figures come from TechCrunch's named interviews and an unnamed person familiar with GIC's finances.

The investors who backed Anthropic before anyone else believed in it are now sitting on one of the great venture wins of the decade — and their bets explain a lot about how the AI race was financed.

Notable Capital, the firm formerly known as GGV Capital before its 2024 split from its Chinese operations, put roughly a tenth of the American share of its billion-dollar-plus fund into Anthropic's Series E round. The move baffled many of the firm's own limited partners. Claude was still running behind OpenAI's ChatGPT, DeepSeek had released its R1 model only weeks earlier, and the $61.5 billion valuation was the richest the early-stage firm had ever paid in 25 years of investing.

The team behind the bet — managing partners Hans Tung and Glenn Solomon and principal Dan Cahana — had run the numbers and found that software developers were turning to Claude over ChatGPT about 70 percent of the time, a reversal from the year before. Tung, a co-founder of the firm, told TechCrunch that developers sit at the center of AI because they are its earliest adopters. The firm also reasoned, drawing on its own experience navigating U.S.-China tensions, that the most sensitive work would always need American-built, closed models.

Less than two years later, that conviction has paid off handsomely. Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in the largest public offering in history on Wednesday, pricing at a fully diluted value of about $1.5 trillion. Notable's stake is worth roughly 15 times its investment on paper.

It was not the only firm to see it coming. Singapore's sovereign wealth fund, GIC, spent 2024 studying Anthropic before its technology chief, Chris Emanuel, met chief financial officer Krishna Rao for dinner at Che Fico, an Italian restaurant in San Francisco, in early 2025. Rao showed him how improvements to the models were pulling in more corporate customers, and GIC made its first investment in the Series F round that September, at a $183 billion valuation. Claude Code, the company's coding tool, had just launched, and Emanuel could already see developers embracing it.

GIC kept doubling down, leading the Series G round alongside Coatue at $380 billion and co-leading the final pre-IPO round at $965 billion. All told it has put more than $4.5 billion into Anthropic across three rounds, leaving it with about 1 percent of the company after the listing, according to a person familiar with its finances.

Baillie Gifford, the investment manager, followed a similar path. Lilian Li, who runs its U.S. growth investments, spent nearly two years studying the company before its first check in the Series F. What convinced her, she said, was that Anthropic kept delivering on its plans while producing strong revenue and healthy gross margins — expanding into legal work and life sciences, and building broader products like Cowork and cybersecurity tools. Baillie Gifford has invested $1.5 billion across three rounds.

The three firms are only the most visible of the winners. Dozens of other backers who bought in at valuations that once looked absurd are now holding stakes worth far more — at least on paper.
