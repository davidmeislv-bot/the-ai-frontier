Ant Group just put $100 million behind a 16-month-old humanoid startup worth $1.6 billion

Simplexity Robotics has never shipped a robot the public has seen — but Ant Group, Alibaba and Tencent are all backing the former Li Auto team's bet on embodied AI.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 22, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The round: Ant Group led a $100 million pre-Series A investment in Simplexity Robotics, a Chinese humanoid-robot startup founded in July 2025, at a $1.6 billion valuation.
  • The people: Simplexity was founded in Hangzhou by three former Li Auto executives; Wang Kai, the former Li Auto chief technology officer, is chairman.
  • The backers: Alibaba, Tencent, HongShan, and Lanchi Ventures all invested again; Ant has now backed 12 humanoid companies since the start of 2025.
  • The caveat: All figures trace to Bloomberg's reporting and the chairman's confirmation; Simplexity has not shipped a robot, though it has unveiled a wheeled humanoid, iX.

Ant Group has led a $100 million investment in Simplexity Robotics, a Chinese humanoid-robot startup that is barely sixteen months old and is now valued at $1.6 billion, Bloomberg reported on Sunday. The company's chairman, Wang Kai, confirmed the round to Bloomberg.

Simplexity was founded in July 2025 in Hangzhou by three former executives of Li Auto, the Chinese electric-vehicle maker. Wang, Li Auto's former chief technology officer, chairs the company; Jia Peng, who ran the carmaker's self-driving research, is chief executive; and Wang Jiajia, who oversaw mass production of its driver-assistance systems, is chief operating officer. The startup sells combined hardware, software and self-driving systems to business customers in manufacturing and smart vehicles.

The round was a pre-Series A — an early-stage financing, but a large one. Existing investors Alibaba, Tencent, HongShan Capital and Lanchi Ventures all added money alongside Ant. A $1.6 billion valuation for a company founded last summer shows how fast capital is moving into China's humanoid race, where dozens of startups are competing to build robots that can work in factories and warehouses.

For Ant, the Jack Ma-backed financial technology group, the investment is its twelfth in China's humanoid sector since the start of 2025, according to PitchBook data cited by CNBC in July. Its portfolio already includes stakes in finished-robot makers such as Unitree and Galaxea, parts and software startups including Linkerbot, and its own robotics arm, Shanghai Ant Lingbo Technology, which showed off a humanoid called R1 in September.

One caution: the deal rests on a single source — Bloomberg's reporting, with the valuation and round details coming from the company's chairman — and Simplexity has not published details of its customers or how it will spend the money. Bloomberg's account frames the bet as Ant backing Simplexity to challenge the more established names in a crowded field. The company has unveiled a wheeled humanoid, iX, that it says can serve drinks, deliver items, and lead tours, but it has not shipped a robot.
