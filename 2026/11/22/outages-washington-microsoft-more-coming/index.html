After a week of failures, Washington and Microsoft say the outages aren't over

A national flight halt, a days-long email blackout, and a bank lockout in one week — and the officials watching the systems say more breakdowns are coming.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 22, 2026 · 1 min read

The story in 30 seconds

  • The week: The FAA halted departures nationwide, Microsoft's Outlook stayed dark for days, and JPMorgan customers were locked out of their accounts — all within about a week.
  • The warning: Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN the public should be prepared for more investment in cybersecurity and more bumps as technology changes; Microsoft president Brad Smith told CBS to expect months of disruption.
  • The cause: The week's failures were believed to be cyberattacks, and Smith said AI has made it easier to find weaknesses in software.
  • The pattern: Attacks on government and commercial systems have picked up, with the Agriculture Department, JPMorgan, and supermarket chains among the targets.

Three of the country's most depended-on systems broke in the space of about a week. The Federal Aviation Administration halted departures nationwide. Microsoft's Outlook email stayed dark for days, freezing entire companies. JPMorgan Chase customers found themselves locked out of their bank accounts. The people whose job it is to watch this kind of failure say the run is not finished.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN the public should brace for the bill. "The American people should be prepared for a great deal of investment in upgrading our systems; they should be prepared for investment in cybersecurity, and they should be prepared that any time there is a step change in technology, a step change in both compute and applications, there will be bumps in the road," he said.

Microsoft president Brad Smith struck a sharper note with CBS. "I think we have to be prepared, frankly, for the coming months because this isn't the type of thing that just gets done in a couple of weeks … The whole information infrastructure of the world is in effect being upgraded right now," he said.

The week's failures were believed to be cyberattacks. Smith added a twist: artificial intelligence has made it easier to find weaknesses in software. That knowledge can go two ways — toward fixing the code, or toward breaking into it.

The pattern stretches back months, not days. Attacks on government and commercial systems have picked up, with the Agriculture Department, JPMorgan, and supermarket chains among the targets. Each one is a reminder that the systems people rely on — flights, email, money — run on infrastructure that is old, connected, and under attack.

Neither Clayton nor Smith offered a date when things calm down. The message from both was the same: the upgrades will take months, cost real money, and the failures in between are part of the price.
