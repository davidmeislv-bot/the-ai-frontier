UNLV’s Gagamba is building a drone to wash high-rise windows on the Strip

A UNLV student team is working on an AI-guided drone with a water tank, a robotic brush and a squeegee for cleaning high-rise windows. It is a pre-commercial prototype — the team is building toward a prototype and looking for local business partners for testing.

By Wire Willow Staff · Nov 13, 2026 · 3 min read

The idea starts with a familiar Las Vegas sight: towers of glass that need cleaning, floor after floor. A team of UNLV students working as Gagamba is building an AI-guided drone meant to do that window cleaning from the air instead of from a scaffold.

The machine they describe carries its cleaning kit with it: a water tank, a robotic brush and a squeegee. The AI’s job, in the team’s design, includes deciding how much water to use on each window as it works.

The status needs to be stated plainly: this is a pre-commercial prototype. The team is building toward a prototype and is looking for local business partners for testing. It is not a product readers can hire today, and no testing partner is announced in the reporting.

The work has already earned campus recognition — the team took first place in UNLV’s 2026 President’s Innovation Challenge. The next step to watch is the practical one: whether local businesses step forward as testing partners so the prototype can be tried on real buildings.
